BDC: What the Business Development Bank of Canada Does

A Canadian owner’s guide to BDC’s role, services and financing process.

BDC is the Business Development Bank of Canada, a federal Crown corporation devoted to Canadian entrepreneurs. It provides business financing, advisory services and capital, with a focus on small and medium-sized businesses. The Government of Canada is its sole shareholder, and BDC operates at arm’s length from government. Source: BDC, Who we are.

If you searched for “BDC bank”, the useful starting point is its role: helping owners develop their businesses. BDC complements private-sector financial institutions. It is separate from the Bank of Canada and does not offer everyday deposit banking. Understanding that distinction helps you direct a financing enquiry to the right place.

Use this guide alongside our business finance glossary and business loans in Canada guide. Official sources were checked on 29 October 2026; confirm current product requirements with BDC before applying.

What does BDC do?

BDC’s published overview groups its support into financing, consulting and smart capital. These are different ways to address business needs, so identify which service matches your project before starting a conversation. Source: BDC overview.

Business financing

BDC offers business loans for needs such as equipment, working capital, commercial real estate and business purchases or transfers. Its financing menu also includes support for young businesses. Each product has its own purpose and requirements; a listing does not establish that a particular business qualifies. Source: BDC financing.

Advisory and consulting services

BDC’s consulting services address management challenges, including strategy, finance, operations, leadership, sustainability, marketing and sales. This is advice for running and developing a business. An owner seeking operational help should distinguish that request from an application to borrow money. Source: BDC consulting.

Venture capital and BDC Capital

BDC Capital provides investment and specialised financing. Its published offerings include venture capital for innovators, growth and transition capital, and minority equity investments for mid-market growth companies. A capital investment is a different financing route from an ordinary business loan, with its own investment focus. Source: BDC Capital.

BDC vs a chartered bank

BDC describes itself as complementary to private-sector institutions. The comparison below explains that broad role; it is not a ranking of lenders or a prediction about an application. A chartered bank’s products and lending criteria depend on the institution.

BDC’s mandate statement, startup financing page and ISED’s explanation of lender discretion support these distinctions. BDC’s general FAQ also confirms that it does not accept deposits or provide regular banking services. Owners still need to arrange their everyday banking separately.

BDC vs the Canada Small Business Financing Program

BDC is an institution. The Canada Small Business Financing Program (CSBFP) is a federal program that shares lending risk with financial institutions. They should not be treated as interchangeable names for a government business loan. Source: ISED program overview.

Under the CSBFP, owners present their business proposal to a participating financial institution. The lender makes the credit decision and provides the funds. ISED administers the program but does not accept or process loan applications. Participation is the individual institution’s decision, according to ISED’s small-business FAQ.

Do not assume that a BDC loan is a CSBFP loan because both have a federal connection. The official sources checked for this guide did not establish BDC’s participation as a CSBFP lender. Confirm the delivery route with the institution and use ISED’s lender information. Our CSBFP guide explains the program separately.

How owners typically engage BDC

BDC’s financing FAQ describes preparing financial statements, a business plan and cash flow projections, then applying online with business and financial details. An advisor reviews the submission and contacts the applicant to discuss options.

  1. Check the relevant product. Read its current requirements and identify the project you want to finance.
  2. Submit the online request. Provide the requested business information and explain your financing need.
  3. Discuss the project. BDC’s published startup process says a representative calls to discuss the project and the documents needed for analysis.
  4. Review any offer. That process describes an offer only if the application is approved; submitting a request is not a commitment to lend.

This sequence follows BDC’s published startup application process. Requirements can differ between products. Use the representative’s document request to guide your submission instead of assuming that every applicant needs an identical package.

Where a business plan fits

Lenders, including BDC, use business plans and projections to understand a financing proposal. BDC’s loan proposal guidance identifies the plan as a key part of the proposal. It covers the business, management experience, marketing, operations and financial information.

For preparation, connect the financing request to a specific use of funds. Explain what the business sells, who buys it, how it operates and how management will carry out the project. Support forecasts with research and documents where available. The plan should let a reader follow the reasoning from the project to the financial results.

BDC’s business loan preparation guide discusses cash flow forecasts and says a bank may request projections both with and without the proposed loan. Keep assumptions realistic and distinguish expected sales from money already earned. Ask the lender which forecast period and supporting records it needs.

As a practical preparation step, keep the written story and the forecast consistent. If the plan describes hiring, equipment purchases or a new location, show the corresponding costs and timing in the projections. Label assumptions so the reader can see what depends on future events. Keep supporting material accessible for follow-up questions. This is planning guidance, rather than a separate BDC eligibility rule; the product terms and the lender’s own requests determine what you must submit.

A clear plan supports assessment; it does not guarantee financing, a particular amount or an approval timetable. If you need help organising the narrative and projections, explore our business plan writing services. The financing decision remains with the lender.

Frequently asked questions

Is BDC a government bank?

Yes. BDC is a federal Crown corporation whose sole shareholder is the Government of Canada. It operates at arm’s length and serves entrepreneurs. Source: BDC.

Does BDC give grants?

No. BDC describes its business financing as repayable with interest. Its general FAQ directs grant and subsidy enquiries to the Government of Canada’s Business Benefits Finder. Source: BDC general FAQ.

Can startups apply?

Yes, subject to the relevant product’s requirements. BDC’s published startup loan requires at least 12 consecutive months in operation and revenue generation, alongside other criteria. A pre-launch business should not assume it qualifies for that product. Source: BDC startup financing.

What is the difference between BDC and Futurpreneur?

BDC serves entrepreneurs across business stages; Futurpreneur focuses on young entrepreneurs with financing and mentoring. Source: BDC’s Futurpreneur information.
