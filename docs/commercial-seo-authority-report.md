# Commercial SEO authority report

**Audit date:** 2026-08-31<br>
**Scope:** 222 root-level HTML files, the four commercial destination pages, supporting guides, financing/lender resources, industry clusters, case studies, FAQs, glossary, city pages, entity pages, sitemap, robots rules, redirects, structured data and internal links.

## 1. Executive summary

The audit confirms that the site's intended commercial keyword ownership is already substantially established: the homepage owns national writer/consultant intent, `/services.html` owns service and preparation intent, and the Vancouver and Toronto pages own their respective local writer intent. This pass preserved the commercial targeting introduced in the earlier SEO work and the Organization–Person graph introduced in the AEO work.

The principal opportunity was not another round of title or H1 changes. The four destination pages already have unique titles, descriptions, self-referencing canonicals and one H1. They also cover research, projections, use cases, process, location and professional oversight. Instead, this pass:

- added decision-support content to `/services.html` explaining when professional preparation is more appropriate than an early template or internal draft;
- made the service deliverable more explicit without promising a standard scope for every engagement;
- clarified on each city page when professional support is useful and how scope is defined;
- added two contextual links from distinct York Region pages to the Toronto destination, bringing its focused local-support set in line with the intended range;
- retained the established focused authority links to the homepage, services and Vancouver destinations; and
- documented a sitemap mismatch and off-site authority work that cannot be solved in static page copy.

These changes do not guarantee rankings. Their intended effect is clearer intent ownership, stronger contextual authority flow, more complete decision-making information and lower cannibalization risk.

## 3. Existing cannibalization identified

1. **Homepage versus services:** Earlier work already replaced an overlapping homepage services-focused H2 with national writer/provider language. The current homepage appropriately owns writer/consultant identity while the services page owns packages, deliverables and preparation intent. This separation was preserved.
2. **City portfolio:** Many legitimate city pages use “Business Plan Writers” in titles, but unique cities and local business evidence distinguish their geographic intent. The Toronto page remains the destination for Toronto proper/GTA-centred searches; the Vancouver page remains the destination for Vancouver/Lower Mainland searches.
3. **Supporting pages with commercial terms:** Industry, lender, FAQ, case-study and educational pages use service language, usually once in a relevant CTA or boilerplate resource block. They do not have titles/H1s aimed at the national services cluster. No useful language was removed merely to reduce counts.
4. **Bank-loan intent:** `/small-business-financing-canada.html` is the informational financing hub, `/bank-loan-business-plans.html` is the lender-plan commercial page, and lender pages explain institution-specific preparation. They should remain separate unless Search Console shows persistent query-to-page conflict.
5. **Template intent:** `/sample-other-plan.html` currently owns Canadian template/example intent. A future interactive tool must have materially different functionality and intent rather than becoming a synonym page.
6. **Executive-summary cluster:** Five closely related blog pages remain a potential informational cannibalization cluster. They were not merged without Search Console URL/query evidence.

## 4. Changes made to each destination page

### Homepage (`/`)

No new copy was required. It already answers who the firm serves, what the firm produces, why a professional writer is useful, the five-stage process, research and projection involvement, financing/investor/acquisition use cases, national delivery and founder-led differentiation. Its national title, H1, answer-first content, Atul Jagga attribution and stable entity IDs were preserved.

### Services (`/services.html`)

Added a concise “When professional plan preparation is useful” section. It distinguishes an early template/internal draft from an engagement in which an external reader must test market evidence, financing, operations and forecasts together. It identifies complexity signals (acquisition, multiple revenue streams, a location decision, limited history and a coordinated deadline) and states that the decision and complexity determine scope. A companion deliverables card now explains that the proposal can define a customized plan, cited research, integrated model, assumptions and review cycle.

### Vancouver (`/vancouver-business-plan-writers.html`)

Added a short decision paragraph beneath the existing deliverables. It explains that professional involvement is most useful when external review or the interaction of location, funding and forecasts creates complexity, and that the agreed scope defines the research, model, written deliverables and review cycle. Existing Vancouver/Lower Mainland evidence, remote-delivery disclosure and no-office language were preserved.

### Toronto (`/toronto-business-plan-writers.html`)

Added a distinct decision paragraph beneath the Toronto deliverables. It explains when a lender, investor or management team may benefit from testing location and assumptions together, lists the scope components, and expressly avoids forcing a standard package on every Toronto business. Existing Toronto/GTA evidence and the accurate statement that the firm serves Canada from Toronto were preserved.

- **Professional versus DIY decision:** Services now explains when an early template can be sufficient and when integrated professional work becomes useful.

- No additional FAQ or schema nodes were added merely to repeat commercial phrases.
- No city statistics or time-sensitive program claims were introduced; current local pages already provide meaningful regional considerations and verification warnings.
- No broad rewrite was made to the homepage, which already satisfies the identified commercial decision questions.
- No competitor wording or page structure was copied. The supplied Semrush evidence informed prioritization; live competitor retrieval was unavailable in the execution environment, so no unsupported claim about a competitor's current page was used.

## 12. Remaining off-site SEO requirements

Internal relevance alone is unlikely to close every competitive authority gap. Remaining work includes:

1. Earn editorial links from Canadian entrepreneurship, accounting, financing-education, chamber, incubator and credible industry resources—without paid-link schemes or lender-affiliation implications.
2. Promote evidence-led guides and original practitioner insights as linkable resources rather than requesting links directly to sales copy in every case.
3. Maintain consistent Organization and founder references on verified professional profiles and relevant directories.
4. Build first-party proof through permissioned case studies with useful process detail; never invent outcomes or disclose confidential information.
5. Use digital PR selectively around genuinely new research, tools or data—not generic announcements.
