Match the Plan to the Financing Decision

A business plan for financing must connect the request to the business. We begin by defining how much financing is required, its intended use and the owner's investment. We then connect the business model, market opportunity and operating assumptions to financial projections, cash-flow capacity and the proposed debt service.

Every engagement is customized to the business, transaction and intended recipient. Requirements vary, so we confirm the lender's current checklist and requested format before finalizing scope. Professional preparation can improve clarity and consistency, but it cannot guarantee approval.

Practical lender questions

What Your Business Plan Needs to Help the Lender Assess

The plan should give the reviewer a concise path through the request and the evidence behind it. It helps the lender assess:

  • Borrower and management: ownership, relevant experience and capability.
  • Amount requested: the facility and total financing need.
  • Use of funds: where the money goes and when it is required.
  • Owner contribution: cash or other investment committed to the project.
  • Market opportunity: customers, demand and competition.
  • Historical performance: results and trends for an existing business.
  • Financial assumptions: the operating drivers behind the forecast.
  • Cash-flow and repayment capacity: whether forecast cash can service obligations.
  • Risks and contingencies: key uncertainties and management responses.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Bank Loan Business Plans

Do banks require a business plan for a business loan?

Requirements vary with the lender, borrower, transaction, financing product and amount. Ask the intended lender for its current document checklist before submission.

What should a business plan for a bank loan include?

It should normally explain the financing request, use of funds, owner contribution, market, operations, management, risks and financial projections, adjusted to the lender's requirements.

How many years of financial projections should be included?

Requirements differ. Near-term monthly cash flow plus multi-year annual forecasts are commonly useful, but the period and detail should match the facility and lender request.

Can you prepare a business plan for an existing business?

Yes. The scope can incorporate historical financial results, current operations, the proposed change and future financing requirements.

Can you prepare a plan for buying an existing business?

Yes. An acquisition plan can address the purchase structure, normalized historical results, buyer experience, transition, working capital and post-close debt service.

Do you prepare financial projections as part of the business plan?

Yes, when included in the agreed scope. The model is developed with the narrative so operating assumptions, funding and debt payments remain connected.

Can you guarantee that the bank will approve my loan?

No. Approval is the lender's decision. A professional plan can present the request clearly, but cannot control eligibility, credit, security or approval.

Which Canadian banks do you prepare business plans for?

Plans can be prepared for applications involving Canadian banks, credit unions, government-supported programs and other lenders, subject to the intended recipient's requirements. We are independent and not affiliated with those institutions.
