Logistics Business Loan

Prepare a lender-ready financing request with assumptions grounded in how a logistics operation actually works.

Reviewed by The Biz Plans editorial teamUpdated July 202612-minute guide

Frame the logistics loan request

State the amount, borrower, owner contribution, requested term and exact uses. Common uses include racking, material-handling equipment, systems, deposits and contract ramp-up capital. Match long-lived assets to an appropriate term and identify taxes, deposits and working capital that may need separate funding.

Show repayment capacity

Lenders need a traceable bridge from orders, pallets, storage days, picks, deliveries, utilization and contract pricing to gross profit, operating cash flow and debt payments. Present monthly figures through the ramp-up and seasonal cycle. Reconcile the debt schedule to the income statement, cash flow and balance sheet; adding a loan to cash without recording principal and interest is a common model error.

Build a lender evidence file

  • Owner résumés, ownership structure and personal contribution
  • Supplier quotations, purchase agreements and lease terms
  • Licences or a dated path covering transport, customs, dangerous-goods, warehouse safety, privacy and trade requirements
  • Contracts, letters of intent, pipeline evidence and pricing support
  • Historical statements and tax records for an existing business
  • Base, downside and delayed-start cash-flow scenarios

Stress-test the request

Model slower sales, lower utilization, margin pressure and a delayed opening. Explain the management response: defer hiring, stage purchases, reduce owner draws, renegotiate supply or retain a larger reserve. A downside case is useful when it changes a decision, not when it simply makes every number smaller.

Important: Requirements and costs vary by location and business model. Confirm current rules with the responsible regulator and obtain professional legal, tax and financing advice for your situation.
