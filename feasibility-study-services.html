Feasibility Study Services

Evidence for your next business decision.

Feasibility study services help you test a business concept before committing capital, signing a lease or developing a full business plan. A feasibility study consultant brings market evidence, operating requirements and financial assumptions into one assessment. The purpose is to give you a basis for deciding whether to proceed, revise the concept or walk away. Those decisions remain yours, informed by the evidence and the limits of what is known.

The Biz Plans is Toronto-based and serves clients remotely, including entrepreneurs evaluating US business opportunities. We examine how demand, pricing, capacity, startup costs and cash requirements fit together, with scope agreed around your specific decision. A study separates supported facts from assumptions that still need testing. It also identifies questions to resolve before the next commitment. If you are comparing locations, considering a purchase or testing an immigration business concept, start by explaining what you need to decide and request a free quote.

What a feasibility study includes

Professional feasibility study services examine the business as a connected system. An attractive market is only part of the case: the proposed operation must be capable of serving customers at costs and prices that make sense. The scope should identify the questions to investigate, source material available and gaps that could affect the conclusion. Each component below contributes to the same decision rather than becoming an isolated chapter.

Market feasibility: demand, competition and pricing

Market analysis asks who would buy, why they would choose the offering and what alternatives they already use. Research considers the relevant customer group, geographic market, competitors and pricing evidence. Broad industry growth does not establish demand for a particular location or concept. The assessment connects research to your proposed positioning, sales channels and revenue assumptions, distinguishing observed evidence from estimates about future customer behavior.

Sources and dates matter because a current competitor price, a customer discussion and a national market estimate answer different questions. We look for a clear explanation of how the proposed business would reach its customers. Our market research guide explains the research foundations behind that work.

Technical and operational feasibility

The operating assessment considers location, equipment, capacity, staffing, suppliers and other requirements needed to deliver the offering. A site may look attractive while imposing constraints on access, space or throughput. Supplier quotations, proposed rent and wage assumptions provide more useful inputs than unsupported allowances. Identify any requirements that need confirmation with the appropriate local specialist before treating them as settled facts.

The analysis checks whether the sales case agrees with operational capacity. If growth requires additional equipment, staffing or space, the model should reflect those commitments and their cash effects. A feasibility assessment can identify unresolved technical questions; specialized engineering, legal or site investigations should be scoped separately where needed.

Financial feasibility

Financial analysis brings startup costs, revenue drivers, operating expenses and working capital into projections that can be reviewed together. Break-even analysis asks what level of activity covers the relevant costs. Cash-flow analysis examines when money is received and paid, including expenditure before revenue begins. A projected profit does not by itself explain how the business will meet its obligations while it builds sales.

Sensitivity analysis tests changes in assumptions such as prices, sales volume, input costs or collection patterns. The purpose is to show which variables matter most and what a weaker case would mean for cash needs. Any example figures used in a report should be labeled illustrative; project assumptions should be identified and traced to their sources. Explore our financial modeling services and financial feasibility study guide for related context.

Organizational and management feasibility

The study considers who will run the business, what experience is available and which responsibilities remain uncovered. Staffing assumptions should match the operating plan, including the owner's role and any specialist support required. An appealing concept may still need a different management structure or additional capabilities. Identifying those gaps makes the analysis more useful than assuming every role can be filled without cost or preparation.

A go/no-go recommendation framework

The findings bring the evidence, assumptions, risks and unresolved questions into a recommendation framework. It may support proceeding under stated conditions, revising the concept or stopping further investment. Conditions should be explicit: what evidence is missing, which assumptions are material and what must change before you reconsider. The report supports your judgment and records the reasoning; a favorable finding is never a predetermined deliverable.

Feasibility study vs. business plan

A feasibility study tests whether an idea works under stated assumptions. A business plan documents how the proposed business will work: its market, operations, management, funding needs and financial projections. The documents can share research, but they serve different decisions. A study belongs at the point where you are still questioning the concept. A plan develops the operating and financial case for an intended reader once you have chosen a direction.

Our business plan writing services build the narrative and financial model for lenders, investors and other audiences. A feasibility study may reveal that you should change the location, reduce the initial scope or obtain stronger evidence before commissioning that work. Moving directly to a polished plan can obscure these unanswered questions if the underlying assumptions have not been challenged.

For a US lending context, see our SBA business plan page. For immigration-related planning, the existing immigration business plan services, E-2 business plan, L-1 business plan, EB-2 NIW business plan and EB-5 business plan service pages describe distinct planning contexts. A feasibility assessment tests the underlying business concept; application-specific documentation requires its own scope and current requirements.

Keep evidence from the study available when developing a plan. Research sources, cost quotations and model assumptions should remain traceable, while unresolved issues should stay visible. Reusing the work responsibly means updating assumptions when the concept changes rather than carrying an earlier conclusion into a different project.

Who needs a feasibility study?

The useful trigger is a consequential decision with unanswered questions. You may have enough information to describe a concept but insufficient evidence to commit to it. A focused study helps organize those uncertainties before you take on obligations that are difficult to reverse.

Prospective business buyers and franchisees

A buyer needs to distinguish the existing operation from the assumptions behind the purchase case. A franchisee needs to examine the proposed location, local competition, cost structure and management requirements. The assessment considers how the business would operate under your ownership. It complements, rather than replaces, transaction diligence and specialist review. Our franchise planning page provides a related next step.

Developers evaluating a project or location

Before committing to a site or development concept, examine the relationship between demand, capacity, investment and operating costs. Keep project requirements that need independent confirmation visible. Comparing alternatives on consistent assumptions can be more informative than developing a detailed case for a single preferred option.

Entrepreneurs before a lease or loan application

A lease, equipment purchase or financing request can turn an idea into a substantial commitment. Test whether expected demand and pricing can support the operating structure first. The study helps you identify what you know, what remains uncertain and what additional research would change the decision.

Immigration applicants testing a business concept

Applicants considering a proposed venture may need to test its commercial logic before developing a detailed plan. Market evidence, operational practicality and financial assumptions deserve examination on their own terms. Separate the business decision from the requirements of any particular immigration pathway and discuss those requirements with the appropriate adviser.

How it works with a feasibility study consultant

The sequence follows the site's established approach of discovery, research, financial modeling and review. The emphasis here is testing a concept and documenting findings. Scope depends on the decision, the business's complexity and the information available.

  1. Discovery. Explain the concept, proposed market, intended investment and decision you face. We identify the questions the assessment must answer and discuss available records, quotations and research. Clarifying the purpose helps distinguish a focused feasibility assessment from a full business plan or ongoing finance engagement.
  2. Research and data gathering. Gather business inputs and market evidence from named sources. Proposed rent, equipment quotations, staffing requirements, pricing and customer information help ground the analysis. Missing inputs are identified as assumptions or research gaps so that their effect can be considered.
  3. Market and financial analysis. Connect demand and competition to the operating model and projections. Review startup requirements, break-even, cash needs and sensitivity to material assumptions. Check that revenue expectations agree with staffing and capacity instead of treating the forecast as a separate exercise.
  4. Findings and recommendation report. Review the evidence, assumptions, limitations and recommendation framework. Your input helps check whether the analysis reflects the proposed operation. The report gives you a basis for deciding whether to proceed, revise or walk away, with unresolved questions made explicit.

Before work begins, confirm the deliverables, information responsibilities and review arrangements in the proposal. Research availability and the readiness of your inputs influence the working sequence. Discuss any external deadline during discovery so the scope and schedule can be assessed together.

Feasibility study services: frequently asked questions

What is included in a feasibility study?

The agreed scope can include market demand, competition and pricing; location, capacity and operating requirements; startup costs, projections, break-even and sensitivity analysis; management capabilities; and a findings report with a go/no-go recommendation framework. The report should distinguish evidence from assumptions and identify unresolved questions. Confirm which research, models and specialist inputs are included before work begins.

How is a feasibility study different from a business plan?

A feasibility study tests whether a concept is workable under stated assumptions and supports your decision to proceed, revise or walk away. A business plan explains how the chosen business will operate and presents its narrative and financial case for an intended audience. Study findings can inform a later plan, but a plan should reflect any changes to the concept or evidence.

Who should get a feasibility study before starting a business?

Consider one when a substantial commitment depends on uncertain demand, costs or operating assumptions. Situations include buying a business, considering a franchise, evaluating a development, choosing a location, or preparing to sign a lease. Immigration applicants may also want to test a concept before commissioning a detailed business plan. The scope should follow the decision and the gaps in available evidence.

What does a feasibility study cost?

Cost depends on scope, research requirements, project complexity and the information already available. A focused assessment and a broader study involving several alternatives require different work. Request a free quote describing your concept and the decision ahead. Confirm the deliverables, fee basis and any additional work in the proposal; this page does not publish a standard study price.

How long does a feasibility study take?

The schedule depends on research needs, access to source information, complexity and review requirements. A concept with current quotations and organized records may be more straightforward to assess than one with substantial data gaps. Explain any external deadline during discovery. The schedule should be confirmed with the agreed scope rather than assumed from a generic turnaround.
