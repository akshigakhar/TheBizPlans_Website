TAM SAM SOM: What It Means and How to Calculate It (With an Example)

Define your market, test your assumptions and connect opportunity to achievable sales.

TAM SAM SOM meaning: TAM (total addressable market) is the total annual revenue opportunity for a defined offer if every relevant customer bought it. SAM (serviceable available market) is the portion of TAM your offer and geographic reach can serve. SOM (serviceable obtainable market) is the portion of SAM you can realistically win within a stated period, given competition, sales resources and operating capacity.

This guide explains how to calculate TAM SAM SOM, connect it to a sales forecast, and present the evidence in a Canadian business plan. The terms describe different boundaries around the same opportunity.

Illustrative example only: Maple Table is a fictional Toronto meal-prep business. Every customer count, price, percentage, time assumption and financial amount in its example is illustrative, not Canadian market data or a forecast for a real company. All amounts are Canadian dollars, excluding sales tax.

TAM, SAM and SOM: define the boundaries first

A market estimate becomes useful when someone else can reproduce it. Before looking for a large industry total, write down what you sell, who pays, which geography you cover and which period you measure. A prepared-meal subscription, a grocery delivery service and a restaurant may compete for a household's food budget, but they do not automatically share the same addressable market. Define the purchase you are measuring before choosing the dataset.

TAM describes the full opportunity within that definition. It need not mean the entire world: a deliberately Canadian offer can have a Canadian TAM. SAM then applies practical restrictions such as delivery territory, product suitability, customer eligibility and channels. SOM adds the commercial question: how much can this particular business obtain during its planning horizon? Keep that horizon visible so that a mature company's potential is not mistaken for a startup's launch-year sales.

Use a consistent measurement basis across the three layers. Annual revenue is often the clearest choice for a plan, with customer counts and units shown underneath. A household count cannot be compared directly with a dollar forecast. Nor should a national annual spending estimate be compared with monthly local sales without converting the periods. The arithmetic is simple; maintaining consistent definitions is the harder part.

TAM SAM SOM example: a fictional Toronto meal-prep company

Maple Table sells a recurring prepared-meal package to households. For this illustrative model, one active household buys one package each week at an illustrative price of $100 for an illustrative 50 purchasing weeks a year. Annual spending per active household is therefore an illustrative $5,000. This is a modelling assumption, not evidence that every household wants the package or will keep buying it.

Calculate TAM: the defined overall opportunity

Suppose the fictional research model identifies an illustrative 100,000 eligible Canadian households for this exact package. Multiplying that illustrative count by illustrative annual spending of $5,000 gives an illustrative TAM of $500 million annually. In a real plan, the household estimate needs evidence for both the population and the eligibility filters. A population table alone does not prove willingness to purchase a premium meal subscription.

Illustrative TAM = 100,000 households × $5,000/year = $500 million/year

Calculate SAM: the opportunity the offer can serve

Maple Table's launch territory is limited to a defined Toronto delivery area. Applying geography and offer-fit filters leaves an illustrative 10,000 eligible households. At the same illustrative annual spending of $5,000, SAM is an illustrative $50 million annually. The delivery boundary matters: households outside a practical route are excluded even if their income and preferences match the target customer. Keep the filter sequence in a worksheet so overlapping exclusions are not counted twice.

Illustrative SAM = 10,000 households × $5,000/year = $50 million/year

Calculate SOM: reconcile demand with capacity

For the illustrative first full operating year, assume Maple Table can acquire and retain an illustrative average of 200 active households, supported by a documented sales funnel and delivery capacity. The resulting SOM is an illustrative $1 million annually, or an illustrative 2% of SAM. The percentage is an output of the operating model; selecting it first would hide the work needed to win and serve those households.

Illustrative SOM = 200 average active households × $5,000/year = $1 million/year

An illustrative kitchen capacity of 250 household packages per week would leave an illustrative 50-package weekly buffer at that average demand. Capacity still needs a peak-week check: an average does not prove that the busiest delivery day is workable. The plan should address staffing, kitchen access, packaging, delivery windows and cancellations. A marketing budget that attracts customers beyond available production is not a credible revenue model.

Average active customers also differ from customers at year-end. If the company grows gradually, multiplying its ending customer count by a full year's spending overstates revenue. Build monthly customer cohorts, subtract churn and account for pauses before summing annual sales. In this illustrative example, the illustrative 200-household average is a simplifying assumption across the operating year, not a claim that launch-day demand instantly reaches that level.

Top-down vs bottom-up market sizing

A top-down approach starts with a broad published market and narrows it with relevant filters. It can help establish context and test whether the opportunity is plausible. Its weakness is the temptation to multiply a large category total by convenient percentages. Each filter needs a reason and a source: a national food expenditure total includes purchases that Maple Table cannot realistically replace, and an industry revenue figure may include business models outside its offer.

A bottom-up approach starts with identifiable customers, purchase frequency, achievable prices and delivery capacity. For Maple Table, that means mapping the actual route area, validating interest with potential buyers, testing package pricing, estimating acquisition through specific channels and checking production throughput. The result connects market research to the sales forecast rather than leaving a large opportunity number on a slide with no explanation of how revenue arrives.

Lenders have a practical reason to put more weight on a well-supported bottom-up forecast: they can challenge its inputs and follow them through to cash available for repayment. They can ask how customer demand becomes paid orders, what it costs to acquire those orders, and whether the business can fulfil them profitably. A large TAM alone cannot answer those questions. Individual lenders may use different review methods; the labels themselves are not a universal lending requirement.

Atul's planning lens: find the missing bridge

The practical review question is whether the market story and financial model describe the same business. Watch for plans that move directly from a large industry figure to confident sales without explaining customer acquisition, repeat purchases or capacity. Another weak bridge is a detailed customer forecast with no evidence that enough suitable buyers exist in the launch territory. The two approaches should challenge each other and reveal unsupported assumptions.

Document that bridge in the plan: segment definition, reachable buyers, conversion evidence, purchase frequency, price, retention and fulfilment limits. This is the discipline behind our business plan writing services and financial modelling. For the downstream earnings calculation, read the EBITDA glossary and worked example. Market opportunity, revenue and operating earnings answer different questions and need separate calculations.

Canadian sources for market-size research

Start with Statistics Canada for demographic, household and industry context. Its Census of Population can help define local populations and household characteristics. Record the table or profile, reference year, geography and exact variable used. Municipal boundaries and delivery zones may not align, so explain how the source geography is translated into your service area.

IBISWorld Canada offers industry context; use publicly accessible material only for claims it actually supports, and distinguish a public summary from a full report. A report title or headline does not establish a local addressable market. The Government of Canada's business and industry research resources can also help locate relevant evidence. No market statistic from these sources is asserted in this fictional example.

Supplement secondary research with customer interviews, paid pilot orders, competitor pricing and documented supplier or kitchen quotes. Record sample selection and limitations: expressions of interest are weaker evidence than paid purchases, and a convenience sample may overrepresent enthusiastic buyers. Keep an assumptions register identifying which inputs are sourced, directly observed or estimated. Use the Canadian business plan benchmarks guide as a further planning reference while checking whether any comparison fits your business model.

Where TAM SAM SOM shows up in a business plan

Put the core analysis in the market analysis section, after defining the customer and before presenting the sales strategy. Show the nested opportunity, a concise calculation and the key sources. Move detailed filters and research notes to an appendix. Then cross-reference the forecast so readers can follow the path from eligible customers to orders and annual revenue. Our market research guide explains how this evidence fits the wider market assessment.

In a pitch deck, a compact market slide can show the opportunity and initial segment. Follow it with traction, distribution and the operating plan that supports the obtainable share. Investors may discuss long-term expansion, but distinguish that future scope from the geography and product the company serves today. A larger future TAM does not increase the launch team's present production or sales capacity.

In immigration business plans, market sizing helps explain why the proposed business has a viable customer base in its intended Canadian location. Connect the analysis to the applicant's actual offer, operating plan and local research. Requirements depend on the relevant program; a market diagram does not establish eligibility or guarantee an outcome. Keep program-specific claims tied to current official guidance.

For a financing application, link obtainable demand to the financial projections, working capital and downside case. Show what happens if customers arrive more slowly, purchase less frequently or cost more to acquire. Our bank loan business plan service and guide to how banks evaluate plans provide related context. Strong market research supports a repayment story; it does not replace cash-flow analysis.

Five common TAM SAM SOM mistakes

1. Inflating TAM with an unrelated category

Using all Canadian food spending for a narrowly defined meal package makes the opportunity look larger without making it more relevant. Define the substitution you expect customers to make and exclude spending the offer cannot reasonably address. Explain any adjacent category you include. A smaller, well-defined opportunity is easier to defend than a large total assembled from loosely related purchases.

2. Leaving numbers without sources

A precise number is still an assumption when nobody can trace it. Include source names, dates, definitions and calculation steps. For original research, describe who participated and what you asked. If an input is a management estimate, label it and show a sensitivity range. Do not attach a credible source's name to a conclusion that the source never made.

3. Treating SAM as identical to TAM

Apply the restrictions created by your actual offer, channels and eligibility rules. If SAM truly equals TAM, explain why the entire defined market is serviceable. For a local launch, identical figures usually signal a missing filter. Avoid subtracting the same households repeatedly through overlapping criteria; use a clear sequence or a dataset that can apply the criteria together.

4. Ignoring geography

A national opportunity does not establish local demand. Delivery time, customer density, access and local competition affect who can be served economically. Define the initial operating area, then distinguish expansion territories. Maple Table's illustrative Canadian TAM and illustrative Toronto SAM use different geographic boundaries deliberately; the national number is context for an offer whose launch reach remains local.

5. Confusing revenue with units

Households, packages, meals and dollars are different units. Show the conversion from customers to transactions and from transactions to revenue, using consistent periods. Exclude sales tax from revenue assumptions and explain treatment of discounts and refunds. Never compare annual SAM dollars with monthly SOM units. Check that the forecast and market worksheet use the same price and purchase-frequency assumptions.

Before using a market-size estimate to justify sales, check the TAM, SAM and SOM mistakes lenders spot so the reachable customer base and forecast remain consistent.

Frequently asked questions

What does TAM SAM SOM stand for?

TAM stands for total addressable market, SAM for serviceable available market, and SOM for serviceable obtainable market. They describe the total defined opportunity, the part your offer can serve, and the share your business can realistically win within a stated period. Use the same units and timeframe across all three.

How do you calculate TAM?

Define the offer, eligible customer group, geography and period, then multiply eligible customers by annual spending per customer for that offer. Alternatively, narrow a relevant published market total with supported filters. Record the source and logic for each input, avoid overlapping segments, and distinguish estimates from observed purchasing behaviour.

What's a good SOM for a startup?

There is no universal good percentage. A defensible startup SOM follows from reachable customers, acquisition evidence, repeat purchasing and operating capacity over a stated period. Work out achievable revenue first and calculate its share of SAM afterwards. Test a slower-sales case before using the figure to support hiring or borrowing.

Do banks care about TAM SAM SOM?

Banks care about evidence of demand and the ability to repay, although they may not require these exact labels. A supported bottom-up estimate helps connect market research to revenue and cash flow. A large TAM alone does not demonstrate repayment capacity; the operating forecast, margins, working capital and debt payments still matter.

TAM SAM SOM vs market size — same thing?

Market size is a general term for the scale of a defined opportunity. TAM, SAM and SOM separate that opportunity into total, serviceable and obtainable layers. State which layer a market-size figure represents, along with its geography, period and units, so readers do not mistake a broad opportunity for expected sales.

What sources can I use for market-size numbers?

Use Statistics Canada demographic and industry data, relevant publicly accessible IBISWorld Canada material, government research resources and suitable industry publications. Add local customer research, pilot purchases and competitor pricing. Cite the exact source and reference period, explain any filters, and label your own assumptions rather than presenting them as published statistics.

Where does TAM SAM SOM go in a business plan?

Place the main analysis in the market analysis section and connect SOM to the sales strategy and financial projections. Keep detailed sources and calculations in an appendix. Pitch decks can summarize the analysis on a market slide; immigration plans should connect it to the proposed location and business model while following the applicable program guidance.
