Build the investment case

What an Investor Needs to Understand About Your Business

An investor business plan must connect the opportunity, evidence, execution plan and capital requirement—not simply describe the company.

Problem & solution

What genuine market need exists, and why is the offering a compelling response?

Market

How large and attractive is the realistic opportunity for the target customers and geography?

Business model

How does the company make money, and which revenue and margin drivers matter most?

Advantage & traction

Why can the business win, and what customer, revenue, product or partnership evidence exists?

Management & growth

Why can this team execute, and how will investment accelerate a credible growth strategy?

Capital & financial outlook

How much is being raised, where will it go, and what does the growth model imply financially?

Different readers, different emphasis

Investor Business Plan vs. Bank Loan Business Plan

Both audiences care about disciplined management, credible assumptions and financial performance. The difference is emphasis: lenders generally focus more on repayment and downside protection, while equity investors generally focus more on growth and value creation.

Evidence before headlines

Credible Market Sizing: TAM, SAM and SOM

Where appropriate, investor-focused planning distinguishes the total addressable market (TAM), serviceable available market (SAM) and serviceable obtainable market (SOM). Each estimate should use credible sources and reconcile with the company’s actual customers, geography, capacity and go-to-market strategy.

For many Canadian small businesses, a realistic serviceable market analysis is more useful than citing a huge global industry figure with no clear path to customers. We focus on decision-useful evidence rather than manufacturing impressive-looking numbers.

A defensible market case asks:

  • Who can realistically buy?
  • Which regions can the business serve?
  • What supports the demand estimate?
  • What share can capacity and strategy support?
  • Do the market and revenue forecasts reconcile?

Make the assumptions visible

Financial Projections for Investors

The model should tell the investment story by connecting operational drivers to funding needs and future financial position:

Customer and revenue drivers → growth assumptions → gross margins → staffing → marketing → operating expenses → capital expenditure → working capital → cash burn → funding requirement → runway → future financial position

For relevant early-stage businesses, a credible model may show when additional funding is required rather than artificially forcing profitability in Year 1. Assumptions, scenarios and limitations should be transparent.

Capital connected to execution

How the Investment Will Be Used

Potential uses include product development, equipment, premises, inventory, hiring, sales, marketing, technology, working capital, expansion and market entry. The categories and amounts must reflect the actual plan.

A strong use-of-funds schedule connects capital to measurable milestones instead of presenting only a funding number.

Illustrative milestone logic

Investment → hire the planned sales team → enter two defined markets → expand customer acquisition → reach the projected revenue milestone

Know what each document does

Business Plan, Financial Model and Pitch Deck

Business Plan

The detailed business case: opportunity, market, strategy, operations, management, capital needs, risks and financial outlook.

Financial Model

The quantified assumptions, operating drivers, cash requirements, scenarios and projected financial statements.

Pitch Deck

A condensed presentation used in investor conversations. It complements rather than replaces the plan and model. Pitch-deck availability can be discussed during scoping.

A note on valuation: an investor plan can support fundraising discussions, but it does not determine what a company is worth. Formal business valuation is a separate professional exercise.

Investor plan questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is an investor business plan for?

It is for businesses preparing to discuss equity investment, growth capital or another investor-led raise. The appropriate depth depends on stage, audience and transaction.

Is this different from a bank loan plan?

Yes. Both require credible financials, but investor plans generally place greater emphasis on market opportunity, scalability, value creation, capital milestones and upside potential.

Can the engagement include financial projections?

Yes, depending on scope. The model may include integrated statements, cash burn, runway, funding needs and scenarios tied to operating assumptions.

Does a business plan guarantee investment?

No. Investors make independent decisions. A professional plan supports clear, evidence-based discussions but cannot guarantee funding, valuation or performance.
