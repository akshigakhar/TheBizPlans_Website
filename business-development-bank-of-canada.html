BDC Business Loans and Financing

How Canadian business owners can prepare a business plan, financial projections and supporting case for a financing application.

Last reviewed: August 2026. This independent guide focuses on financing preparation, not official product advice. BDC products, terms, eligibility and documentation requirements can change; confirm current information directly with BDC.

The Business Development Bank of Canada (BDC) is a federal Crown corporation that supports Canadian entrepreneurs through financing, advisory services and capital. This page explains how a business plan and financial model can help communicate a financing request; it is not an official BDC page.

BDC Business Financing

BDC describes itself as Canada’s bank for entrepreneurs and works with businesses across industries and stages. Its role differs from that of a conventional retail bank, but each application is still assessed on its own facts. For BDC’s mandate, services and current information, visit the official BDC overview.

Before choosing a financing option, define the amount required, the use and timing of funds, the owner contribution, and the expected business result. Current rates, eligibility, repayment features and application steps should always be checked on BDC’s official financing page.

Types of BDC Business Financing

BDC’s financing information currently organizes lending around common business needs. These can include:

  • Working capital and growth: funds for initiatives such as market expansion, hiring, inventory or other growth-related costs.
  • Equipment and technology: financing for productive assets, automation, software or digital investment.
  • Commercial real estate: financing connected with buying, constructing or improving business premises.
  • Business purchase and transfer: financing for acquisitions, ownership transitions or succession.
  • Specialized situations: options that may be directed to a particular business stage, industry, project or entrepreneur group.

Product names and availability can change. This summary is intentionally non-specific: compare the live options, qualifications and permitted uses on BDC’s financing website before relying on them.

Preparing for BDC Financing

Start with the transaction rather than a generic template. Write down what is being financed, the complete project cost, how much the owners will contribute, when funds are needed, and how the investment is expected to improve the business. An established company should also reconcile its plan to historical results and explain material changes.

Supporting records will depend on the borrower and request. They may include historical and interim financial statements, tax information, debt schedules, ownership information, management résumés, purchase agreements, leases, supplier quotations or evidence supporting the owner investment. Confirm the actual application checklist directly with BDC.

Business Plan for BDC Financing

A financing-focused business plan gives a reviewer a connected explanation of the company, request and repayment case. Depending on the financing request and circumstances, an applicant may need to demonstrate:

  • business model and sources of revenue
  • management experience and responsibilities
  • financing amount and timing
  • purpose and itemized use of funds
  • owner investment and other funding sources
  • historical performance, where applicable
  • market opportunity and competitive position
  • revenue assumptions and gross margins
  • payroll and operating costs
  • existing and proposed debt
  • cash-flow capacity
  • ability to service financing

These are planning considerations, not a statement that BDC requires every item for every application. The scope should follow BDC’s current request and the complexity of the transaction.

Financial Projections for a Financing Application

Financial projections translate the plan into a testable cash-flow case. This is especially important for established businesses, where the forecast should bridge actual historical performance to the changes expected from the proposed financing.

A coherent model connects historical results → forecast revenue → gross margins → payroll → operating expenses → capital expenditures → working capital → financing → debt service → closing cash balance. The assumptions in the written plan should agree with the model, and the income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement should reconcile.

Useful sensitivity cases can show what happens if sales ramp more slowly, margins tighten, costs rise or implementation is delayed. They do not eliminate risk; they help the applicant identify cash pressure and explain possible management responses.

What Lenders Evaluate

Every credit decision is lender-specific. In general, financing reviewers look for a viable borrower, a clearly defined request, credible management, reliable supporting information, sufficient cash generation and risks that are understood and addressed.

  • Borrower and management: relevant experience, ownership, credit history and capacity to carry out the plan.
  • Purpose and structure: a complete use-of-funds schedule and a sensible mix of owner funds and financing.
  • Performance and assumptions: historical trends, evidence behind the forecast and explanations for significant changes.
  • Repayment capacity: cash available after operating needs, working capital, capital spending and existing obligations.
  • Risk and security: downside exposure, available mitigations and any security or guarantees requested by the lender.

For a deeper explanation, read How Banks Evaluate Business Plans. Some eligible asset purchases may also be financed through a participating lender under the Canada Small Business Financing Program (CSBFP); it is a separate program with its own current rules.

Frequently Asked Questions About BDC Financing

Does every BDC financing application require a business plan?

Not necessarily. Documentation depends on the product, borrower and financing request. Confirm the current requirements with BDC. A structured plan can be particularly useful when the reviewer needs to understand a start-up, acquisition, expansion or material change.

What should a BDC business plan include?

Where relevant, explain the business model, management, market, financing amount, use of funds, owner contribution, operating plan, risks and financial projections. Match the scope to BDC’s current document request rather than assuming a universal checklist.

Can an existing business use historical results in its projections?

Yes. Historical results can provide a useful baseline. Reconcile the forecast to recent performance and clearly support changes in revenue, margins, staffing, expenses, working capital and capital spending.

Does a professional business plan guarantee BDC approval?

No. BDC makes its own eligibility and credit decisions. A clear, evidence-based plan can help communicate the financing case, but no consultant can guarantee approval, terms or an amount.

Where can I confirm BDC loan rates and requirements?

Use BDC’s official website or speak directly with BDC. Product names, rates, eligibility, repayment terms and required documents can change, so this independent guide does not reproduce them.

Is The Biz Plans affiliated with BDC?

No. The Biz Plans is an independent business-plan consulting firm. We prepare business plans and financial projections; we do not represent BDC, sell BDC loans or make lending decisions.
