SWOT Analysis Examples: Brands & Small Businesses

Published November 12, 2026

See how Canadian and global brands fit the four SWOT quadrants, then adapt the format to a small business plan.

Worked Examples

What is a SWOT analysis in a business plan?

A SWOT analysis summarises internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats. In a business plan, it explains why the proposed strategy fits the business’s capabilities and market conditions. A strength is something the business already brings; an opportunity is a condition outside the business that it might pursue.

Use these examples to study the level of specificity and the distinction between the quadrants. For research, prioritisation and turning the grid into decisions, read SWOT Analysis: From Data to Strategy.

These are independent educational interpretations of widely known brand characteristics, written for this page in November 2026. They are not company-issued SWOTs or forecasts. Opportunities are possibilities to evaluate, not announced initiatives. Shopify, Tim Hortons, Lululemon and Canadian Tire provide a Canadian-founded mix alongside global technology, entertainment, retail and coffee brands. A founder should use evidence from their own market rather than copy a large company’s advantages.

Shopify SWOT analysis example

Commerce platform · Canadian-founded

Strengths

  • An integrated storefront, checkout and payments ecosystem lets merchants manage connected parts of an online business.
  • An app and partner ecosystem gives merchants options beyond the core platform.

Weaknesses

  • Reliance on third-party apps for specialised needs can add cost and complexity to a merchant’s setup.
  • A platform serving many types of merchants must balance simple onboarding with advanced requirements.

Opportunities

  • Retailers seeking to connect physical and online selling create room for unified commerce tools.
  • Merchants expanding internationally need help adapting checkout, payments and storefront experiences.

Threats

  • Competing commerce platforms and marketplaces give merchants alternative routes to buyers.
  • Changes in payment rules, privacy requirements and online fraud can increase compliance and support demands.

Business plan takeaway: For an online retailer, platform convenience is a supplier benefit, while dependence on that platform belongs in the retailer’s own weaknesses. Test app costs and switching options in the operating plan.

Tim Hortons SWOT analysis example

Coffee and quick service · Canadian-founded

Strengths

  • A familiar Canadian brand and a broad restaurant presence support habitual coffee and breakfast purchases.
  • Coffee, baked goods and meal options offer several reasons to visit throughout the day.

Weaknesses

  • A franchise model requires coordination across independently operated restaurants to deliver consistent service.
  • A broad food and beverage menu adds preparation and training complexity compared with a narrow coffee offer.

Opportunities

  • Customers seeking convenient ordering create opportunities to improve pickup and digital ordering experiences.
  • Interest in cold beverages and portable snacks offers occasions beyond the traditional hot-coffee purchase.

Threats

  • Coffee chains, independent cafés and convenience stores compete for the same everyday purchase.
  • Changes in coffee ingredient costs and local labour availability can put pressure on restaurant economics.

Business plan takeaway: A founder can borrow the focus on a repeat purchase occasion, but should define a local customer need rather than assume a new café has the same brand recognition.

Lululemon SWOT analysis example

Athletic apparel · Canadian-founded

Strengths

  • A recognisable premium activewear identity supports a focused product and store experience.
  • Direct stores and online selling give the brand control over how its products are presented.

Weaknesses

  • Premium pricing limits accessibility for price-sensitive shoppers.
  • Technical apparel and seasonal collections require careful fit, assortment and inventory decisions.

Opportunities

  • Interest in versatile clothing for exercise and everyday wear opens additional use cases.
  • Demand for men’s activewear and new geographic markets offers potential audiences to evaluate.

Threats

  • Sportswear brands and lower-priced alternatives compete for activewear spending.
  • Changing fashion preferences and pressure on discretionary budgets can weaken demand for premium items.

Business plan takeaway: A clothing founder should translate a positioning strength into a precise buyer and product promise, then test whether the price and assortment fit that audience.

Canadian Tire SWOT analysis example

Automotive and general retail · Canadian-founded

Strengths

  • Automotive, tools, home and seasonal categories connect the retailer with practical household tasks.
  • Physical stores and automotive service locations offer in-person access alongside online shopping.

Weaknesses

  • A broad assortment creates inventory and merchandising complexity across very different categories.
  • Bulky and seasonal products require space and careful planning for stock that may sell unevenly.

Opportunities

  • Customers researching products online before collecting locally create opportunities for better stock visibility and pickup.
  • Home maintenance and outdoor projects create demand for useful product bundles and guidance.

Threats

  • Online marketplaces and specialist retailers compete on selection, convenience and price.
  • Weather shifts can disrupt demand for seasonal products, while household budget pressure can delay project purchases.

Business plan takeaway: A small retailer can apply the task-based approach to a narrow assortment. Its forecast should reflect seasonality and storage capacity rather than assume every category sells evenly.

Tesla SWOT analysis example

Electric vehicles and energy · Global

Strengths

  • Vehicle software, charging and energy products provide connected parts of an electric-mobility offer.
  • A distinctive electric-vehicle identity makes the brand easy for prospective buyers to recognise.

Weaknesses

  • Manufacturing and servicing physical vehicles require substantial facilities, parts and operational coordination.
  • A relatively focused vehicle range leaves fewer body-style choices than a broad multi-brand automotive group.

Opportunities

  • Electrification policies and interest in alternatives to combustion vehicles can expand the pool of potential buyers.
  • Demand for energy storage creates applications alongside vehicle sales.

Threats

  • Established automakers and newer electric-vehicle producers compete on price, range and features.
  • Changes to incentives, tariffs and charging standards can alter purchasing decisions and operating requirements.

Business plan takeaway: A founder offering a new technology should separate product capability from external adoption conditions. Model demand with and without policy support rather than treating incentives as permanent.

Netflix SWOT analysis example

Streaming entertainment · Global

Strengths

  • An on-demand service available across devices makes viewing convenient.
  • A catalogue combining original productions and licensed titles supports different viewing preferences.

Weaknesses

  • Creating and acquiring content requires ongoing commitments before audience response is certain.
  • Subscribers can cancel, so the service must keep giving viewers a reason to return.

Opportunities

  • Advertiser interest in streaming audiences creates scope to develop advertising-supported viewing.
  • Interest in stories from different countries can help local productions reach audiences beyond their original market.

Threats

  • Other streaming services, gaming and social video compete for leisure time.
  • Content-rights competition and changing production rules can make programming harder to secure.

Business plan takeaway: A subscription founder should connect retention to a repeatable customer benefit, then allow for ongoing delivery costs and cancellations in the plan.

IKEA SWOT analysis example

Furniture and home furnishings · Global

Strengths

  • Flat-pack products and coordinated room displays support a recognisable self-service furnishing model.
  • Furniture and complementary home accessories let shoppers solve several needs in one trip.

Weaknesses

  • Self-assembly and transporting large purchases place work on the customer.
  • Large-format stores can be less convenient for shoppers who live far away or lack suitable transport.

Opportunities

  • Urban customers furnishing smaller spaces create demand for compact, adaptable products.
  • Interest in repair, reuse and convenient delivery offers additional service possibilities.

Threats

  • Freight disruption and changes in timber or other material costs can affect availability and pricing.
  • Local furniture retailers, online sellers and second-hand marketplaces offer alternative ways to furnish a home.

Business plan takeaway: A founder should spell out the customer tradeoff behind a lower-cost model. If assembly or delivery is excluded, explain how buyers will complete the job.

Starbucks SWOT analysis example

Coffee chain · Global

Strengths

  • A recognisable café brand and customisable drinks create a consistent starting point for customers.
  • Mobile ordering and a loyalty programme connect routine purchases with a digital channel.

Weaknesses

  • Customised drinks can complicate preparation and handoff during busy periods.
  • Premium drink pricing can make frequent visits harder to justify for budget-conscious customers.

Opportunities

  • Demand for cold and personalised beverages creates occasions beyond a standard hot coffee.
  • Customers seeking quick pickup create opportunities to improve ordering and store workflow.

Threats

  • Independent cafés, lower-priced chains and coffee made at home compete for the same spending.
  • Coffee-growing conditions and changes in local labour costs can affect input costs and service delivery.

Business plan takeaway: A café founder should connect the menu to actual preparation capacity. A simpler menu may help serve a specific morning rush before adding customisation.

Fictional small-business SWOT examples

The following businesses, operating details and scenarios are entirely fictional and illustrative. They show how to replace brand-level observations with assumptions a founder can investigate locally.

Fictional coffee shop: Maple Corner Café SWOT analysis example

Illustrative scenario: a proposed neighbourhood café near offices, with a compact kitchen and a founder who has barista experience.

Strengths

  • The founder’s barista experience supports drink preparation and initial staff training.
  • A short coffee-and-pastry menu fits the proposed compact kitchen.

Weaknesses

  • The proposed premises have limited seating and storage, restricting long visits and bulk purchases.
  • The initial roster depends on the founder covering opening shifts, leaving little absence cover.

Opportunities

  • Nearby offices could provide demand for pre-ordered morning pickup; interviews would need to establish interest.
  • Local community events could provide occasions for small catering orders if organisers need suppliers.

Threats

  • An established chain nearby gives commuters a familiar alternative.
  • Office attendance changes could reduce weekday foot traffic; ingredient price changes could squeeze the menu margin.

Business plan takeaway: Validate morning demand with foot-traffic observations and customer interviews, then pilot a limited pickup menu. Arrange backup staffing before accepting catering orders. Compare the coffee shop sample business plan to connect the SWOT to operations and cash-flow assumptions.

Fictional online retailer: North Trail Desk SWOT analysis example

Illustrative scenario: a proposed online shop selling compact desk accessories sourced from a small supplier group.

Strengths

  • The founder has product-photography skills that can support clear size and use demonstrations.
  • A narrow assortment makes initial product descriptions and fulfilment routines easier to manage.

Weaknesses

  • The proposed shop has no established audience, so customer acquisition remains unproven.
  • Depending on a small supplier group limits replacement options if an item becomes unavailable.

Opportunities

  • People furnishing small home workspaces may need compact accessories; interviews and search research can test that need.
  • Relevant workspace newsletters could offer a focused route to buyers if trial campaigns attract suitable traffic.

Threats

  • Marketplace sellers offer comparable accessories with familiar checkout and delivery expectations.
  • Shipping-rate changes, delayed supplier deliveries and paid-ad competition can undermine order economics.

Business plan takeaway: Test a small assortment and acquisition channel before expanding stock. Compare alternative suppliers and calculate delivery and return costs per order. Use the ecommerce sample business plan to connect those checks to the forecast.

How to write your own SWOT: blank template walkthrough

Start with a decision, such as whether to launch a location or add a product line. Define the buyer before listing advantages: the target-market guide and examples, including its target customer and market-sizing example, can help narrow that scope.

Blank SWOT template: replace each blank with your own evidence-backed observation.

Evidence and date: ___
  1. Fill the quadrants. Use specific observations from customer interviews, supplier quotes, competitor research and operating records. Mark untested assumptions clearly.
  2. Select the factors that change the decision. For each priority, complete: “Because ___, we will ___; owner ___; review date ___; evidence to monitor ___.” Pair an opportunity with a capability and a threat with a practical response.
  3. Carry the action into the plan. Reflect staffing limits in operations, acquisition tests in marketing and delivery costs in the forecast. Revisit the grid when evidence changes.
