Canada’s Startup Visa Is Paused: Alternatives

Verified options for founders: provincial entrepreneur streams, intra-company transfer and significant-benefit work permits.

  1. Home
  2. Learning Centre
  3. Startup Visa alternatives

Official program information checked October 29, 2026. This article provides general information, not immigration or legal advice. Business-plan preparation and immigration representation are separate services unless the engagement expressly includes representation.

Startup Visa status: paused to new applications

IRCC’s Start-up Visa Program page states “Status: Paused”. Its Who can apply page says “We’re not currently accepting new applications” and explains that the program was paused on June 30, 2026. IRCC continues processing applications accepted before that date. Both pages display July 21, 2026 as their page date; we checked them on October 29, 2026.

The program landing page still describes an application deadline of June 30, 2026 for holders of valid 2025 commitment certificates. That deadline has passed. Founders starting a fresh application should therefore work from the current paused status, rather than interpret the older certificate wording as an available intake window.

What if you are already mid-plan?

Separate the commercial project from the immigration file. Preserve your research, ownership records, funding evidence and financial model, then ask your authorized representative to assess your actual application stage. A draft plan or earlier discussion with a designated organization should not be treated as confirmation that IRCC accepted an application. Previously accepted files and founders who have yet to apply face different circumstances.

For the site’s existing planning context, see the Start-Up Visa business-plan guide. Before redirecting spending, identify which parts of the venture still make commercial sense and which assumptions depended on SUV. Reusing evidence can be sensible; simply changing the program name on the cover leaves the underlying strategy unresolved.

Alternative 1: provincial entrepreneur streams

A provincial entrepreneur pathway starts with a business and founder that fit a particular province’s requirements. It is not a national replacement for SUV. The official BC and Alberta pages below describe entrepreneur nomination pathways, with permanent residence assessed federally after nomination. An expression of interest, invitation, business application and nomination are different stages; none should be described as an approval promise.

This article names only options verified on current official pages in British Columbia and Alberta. It is a focused shortlist aligned with our existing provincial guides, rather than an exhaustive survey of every province.

British Columbia: Base and Regional

WelcomeBC’s For entrepreneurs and businesses page offers the Entrepreneur Immigration Base stream for starting a business or taking over and growing one. The Regional stream focuses on new businesses in participating communities outside the Metro Vancouver Regional District. Both were presented as application pathways when checked on October 29, 2026. WelcomeBC’s invitations page records invitations in both streams on October 20, 2026, confirming recent activity.

Choose the location for an operating reason: customers, suppliers, staff availability and the founder’s ability to manage locally. A regional proposal should explain why that community needs the business. An acquisition proposal needs evidence about the existing operation and a credible growth strategy. Our BC PNP business-plan guide helps frame the commercial work around the relevant stream.

Alberta: match the stream to your background

Alberta’s AAIP application streams page currently lists four entrepreneur options: Rural Entrepreneur, Graduate Entrepreneur, Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur and Farm. It describes EOI submission for the first three, with designated-agency involvement for foreign graduates, and nomination applications for experienced farmers. These are distinct profiles, rather than interchangeable startup routes.

The Rural Entrepreneur eligibility page explicitly says AAIP is accepting EOIs. Alberta’s August 12, 2026 update also clarifies economic establishment for the Rural, Graduate and Foreign Graduate streams. Consult the AAIP entrepreneur business-plan guide alongside the official requirements for your chosen stream.

For planning, compare a new venture with a purchase using the same discipline: demand evidence, realistic operating costs, ownership, management responsibilities and available capital. This article deliberately avoids investment figures. A published minimum would still need to be checked against the full stream rules and the actual cost of launching or acquiring the proposed business.

Alternative 2: intra-company transfer for an established business

Canadian intra-company transfer, or ICT, may fit an existing foreign business expanding to Canada through a qualifying corporate relationship. IRCC’s current ICT instructions under R205(a), codes C61, C62 and C63 address qualifying multinational enterprises and executive, managerial or specialized-knowledge transfers. They exclude new-business transfers from a foreign enterprise that is not a multinational corporation under this category. Trade-agreement provisions have separate instructions.

This is a temporary work-permit assessment. Owning an overseas company alone does not establish eligibility. Have a representative examine the corporate structure, employment history, proposed duties, temporary assignment and applicable category before commissioning an expansion plan.

The business-plan role is practical: explain what the Canadian operation will sell, who will buy, how it will be funded and staffed, and what the transferred person will actually do. Build a forecast that distinguishes existing foreign revenue from new Canadian sales. Include evidence behind premises, hiring and launch assumptions so the plan can be reconciled with the company’s records.

Our L1 new-office business-plan guide illustrates expansion planning topics such as staffing and organization charts. That page concerns the United States: L1 is not a Canadian permit. Canadian ICT eligibility must be assessed using IRCC’s instructions, even when some commercial planning topics overlap.

Alternative 3: business-owner and significant-benefit work permits

“Owner-operator” describes a way of running a business; it should not be used as the name of a universally available immigration program. The verified Canadian categories discussed here are C11 business owners seeking only temporary residence and C10 significant benefit, both within the International Mobility Program.

IRCC’s C11 business-owner instructions address temporary or seasonal work, separate personal-support and business funds, and significant benefits for Canadians or permanent residents. They explicitly ask officers to examine whether a business plan sets out concrete startup actions. A founder should obtain an eligibility assessment before treating this as a relocation strategy.

IRCC’s C10 significant-benefit guidance remains published and calls for clear, compelling, documented benefit in unique or exceptional situations. Assessment is individual. C10 is not a generic startup substitute or an automatic route to permanent residence.

A useful plan makes the claimed benefit testable. Identify the local problem, the proposed solution, the founder’s contribution and the evidence supporting expected effects. Distinguish commitments from forecasts: an unsigned customer discussion is different from a contract, and a planned position is different from an employee already hired. Explain uncertainty openly instead of attaching immigration outcomes to projected revenue.

Prepare a short decision brief before writing the full plan. Record the proposed business, province, founder experience, ownership, funding sources and desired activity in Canada. Add unresolved eligibility questions for your representative. Then agree on the business-plan scope. This helps keep research and financial modelling connected to a realistic proposal rather than an assumed immigration outcome.

Make the financial model useful for a decision

Build a base case and a slower-sales case before committing to a lease or purchase. Show how cash moves from the funding source into premises, equipment, inventory, staffing and working capital. Keep personal living costs distinct from the company’s operating budget. These are planning recommendations, not additional immigration thresholds.

For example, an expansion forecast should explain whether Canadian customers are already buying from the foreign company or must be acquired from scratch. An acquisition forecast should reconcile the seller’s historical results with changes in wages, financing and management. Record where estimates came from and the date of each quotation. This gives the founder and representative a clearer view of which assumptions need more evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Startup Visa accepting applications?

No. On October 29, 2026, IRCC reported SUV as paused and not accepting new applications. Its eligibility page says applications accepted before June 30, 2026 continue to be processed. Check IRCC again before making decisions.

Can I still move my startup to Canada?

Potentially, if you qualify for another current pathway. Provincial entrepreneur nomination and temporary ICT, C11 or C10 work-permit assessments have different purposes and requirements. Neither incorporating a company nor commissioning a plan establishes immigration eligibility.

Which provinces have entrepreneur streams?

This article verifies British Columbia’s Base and Regional streams and Alberta’s Rural Entrepreneur, Graduate Entrepreneur, Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur and Farm streams. This is a verified shortlist as of October 29, 2026, rather than a complete Canadian list. Check the applicable stream and local participation before proceeding.

Do these routes need a business plan?

Plan requirements depend on the category and stage. Provincial proposals must follow the relevant program instructions; IRCC’s C11 guidance expressly discusses reviewing a business plan. ICT expansion planning and C10 benefit documentation should reflect the applicable official evidence requirements. A generic plan cannot replace eligibility evidence.

Official-source verification

All linked IRCC, WelcomeBC and Alberta program pages were checked on October 29, 2026. SUV: paused; previously accepted applications continue in processing. BC: Base and Regional application pathways listed as active options. Alberta: four entrepreneur streams listed, with Rural explicitly accepting EOIs. ICT, C11 and C10: current IRCC instructions verified. Alberta’s August 12, 2026 update was also checked. Recheck the official pages linked beside each claim before acting; program availability and local participation can change.
