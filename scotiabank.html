Scotiabank Business Loans and Financing in Canada

Business financing information and practical guidance for preparing an application.

Reviewed August 2026. Financing products, eligibility criteria and documentation requirements may change. Confirm current details directly with Scotiabank.

Scotiabank offers business borrowing options for working capital, equipment, property and growth. The right financing structure depends on the purpose, amount, cash-flow cycle and circumstances of the business.

Start with Scotiabank's current loans and leases information, then confirm available products, terms and application documents directly with the bank.

Scotiabank Business Financing Options

Business financing can serve different purposes. A term facility may fund a defined asset or project, while revolving credit may support timing differences between paying suppliers and collecting customer receipts. Scotiabank also publishes information about specialized and government-supported borrowing options.

Product availability, credit limits, rates, security and repayment terms are determined by Scotiabank. Applicants should use the bank's official information and discuss which option fits their intended use of funds.

Business Loans and Lines of Credit

A business loan generally provides a defined amount with scheduled repayments. An application should connect the amount requested to quotations, purchase costs or another documented use and show how the business expects to meet the proposed payments.

A business line of credit is generally used for recurring or short-term working-capital needs. The supporting forecast should show the cash cycle, expected draws and repayments, and peak borrowing need—not only annual profit. Review Scotiabank's current business line-of-credit information.

What You May Need for a Scotiabank Financing Application

The documents requested depend on the product, borrower and transaction. Useful preparation can include identification and ownership records, historical and interim financial statements, existing debt details, tax records, quotations, leases or purchase documents, management backgrounds, and forward-looking projections.

This is general preparation guidance, not a statement of Scotiabank's requirements. Ask the bank which forms, supporting documents, security and guarantees apply to your application.

Business Plan for Scotiabank Financing

A financing-focused business plan should make the request and repayment case easy to follow. Depending on the business and financing purpose, it may establish:

  • the requested financing amount and purpose or use of funds;
  • the owner's contribution and other funding sources;
  • the business background and management experience;
  • the target market, customers and competitors;
  • the basis for sales assumptions and operating costs;
  • existing debt and proposed loan payments;
  • projected cash flow; and
  • repayment capacity under realistic assumptions.

These are general lender and business-plan considerations. They should not be interpreted as items Scotiabank requires in every application. For more preparation guidance, read the Canadian Bank Loan Business Plan Guide and see how banks evaluate business plans and financial projections.

Financial Projections for Scotiabank Business Financing

Financial projections should translate operating assumptions into a connected revenue forecast, expense budget, income statement, cash-flow forecast and balance sheet. A financing model can also incorporate proposed debt payments and sensitivity analysis for variables such as slower sales, delayed collections or higher costs.

Preparing Your Financing Application

  1. Define the facility, amount and intended use of funds.
  2. Confirm Scotiabank's current product and documentation requirements.
  3. Organize financial records, existing debt and evidence of owner contribution.
  4. Gather quotations, agreements and other support for the requested amount.
  5. Reconcile the written plan, sources and uses, projections and proposed payments.

Frequently Asked Questions About Scotiabank Business Financing

Practical answers to help you evaluate this topic and prepare your next step.

What should a business plan for Scotiabank financing include?

Include the amount requested, an itemized use of funds, owner contribution, market evidence, operating plan and integrated financial projections. The assumptions should demonstrate how the business expects to generate enough cash to meet its obligations.

How many years of financial projections are normally useful?

A monthly cash-flow view for the near term and annual projections for later years are often useful, but the appropriate period depends on the lender, program and business stage. Confirm the recipient's current requirements before submitting.

Does a strong business plan guarantee financing approval?

No. The lender or program makes its own eligibility, credit and security decisions. A well-supported plan helps reviewers understand the opportunity and risks, but it cannot guarantee approval or a particular financing amount.

What documents should support the financing request?

Depending on the application, useful records may include owner résumés, quotes, leases, historical statements, tax records, debt schedules, licenses and evidence of equity. Provide only documents relevant to the reviewer and verify current requirements directly.

How should repayment risk be addressed?

Show realistic margins, working-capital needs, debt payments and cash timing. Include sensitivities for important variables such as slower sales, lower pricing or higher costs, then explain the actions management could take.

When should the plan be reviewed before submission?

Review it after the funding structure and supporting quotes are known, and again immediately before submission. Reconcile every funding figure across the narrative, cash flow, balance sheet and use-of-funds schedule.
