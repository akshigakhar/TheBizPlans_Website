What Is a TD Small Business Loan?

TD’s business-financing programs provide entrepreneurs with the capital needed to cover day-to-day operations, purchase equipment, or invest in growth. TD offers both term loans and lines of credit, allowing businesses to choose between fixed repayment schedules or revolving credit for ongoing flexibility. These products can be customized based on your cash-flow cycle, credit profile, and business objectives.

Business Plan for a TD Business Line of Credit

A line-of-credit request should connect the requested limit to the operating cash cycle. The plan should show when inventory, payroll or supplier costs are paid, when customers pay, the expected peak balance and how the line returns toward zero. A term-loan request instead ties a one-time asset or project cost to an amortization and repayment schedule.

Loan Amounts and Terms

While each TD loan is tailored to the borrower’s needs, typical features include:

  • Loan amounts ranging from $10,000 to $1,000,000+ depending on credit strength
  • Repayment terms up to 10 years for term loans and flexible revolving access for lines of credit
  • Fixed or variable interest rates linked to the TD Prime Rate
  • Flexible payment structures aligned with business cash-flow cycles

Preparing a business plan for TD financing

A TD business plan should show why the requested facility matches the funding need. Working-capital lines, term loans, equipment purchases and property projects require different schedules and repayment logic.

Planning a financing application? Start with the facility, use of funds, deadline and records already available.

Request a Free Scope Assessment

What financing reviewers are assessing

  • Clear separation of recurring working capital from one-time capital spending
  • Historical results, management experience and borrower investment
  • Cash conversion, seasonality and peak borrowing for an operating line
  • Project economics and repayment for term debt
  • Asset, security and contingent-cost information relevant to the request

Financial projections to prepare

  • Monthly line utilization or project cash flow
  • Term-loan amortization and debt-service schedules
  • Equipment, renovation or property spending schedule
  • Scenario analysis for slower collections or delayed project benefits

Our CPA-led modelling process reconciles the forecast to the written operating plan and makes the principal assumptions visible for review. The appropriate period and level of detail are confirmed during scope.

Documents to organize before applying

  • Historical and interim financial statements
  • Accounts receivable, inventory or cash-cycle information for working capital
  • Asset quotations, lease or property documentation
  • Existing debt, ownership and management records

Common business-plan weaknesses

  • Calculating a line request without receivable, inventory or payment timing
  • Using a term loan request to cover recurring operating losses
  • Omitting taxes, deposits and soft costs from a capital project
  • Failing to reconcile the requested amount to the forecast cash need

These issues do not describe every credit decision. Eligibility, credit history, security, lender policy and other underwriting considerations remain outside the control of a business-plan writer.

Frequently asked questions

TD business plan FAQs

What should a TD line-of-credit forecast show?

It should show the cash cycle, timing of draws and repayments, and the peak amount needed rather than only annual profit.

Can one plan include a term loan and working capital?

Yes. The purposes, schedules and repayment sources should be separated.

Can the plan address CSBFP?

Yes, when relevant, with eligible project costs distinguished from other cash needs.

Are you affiliated with TD?

No. The Biz Plans provides independent planning support and TD determines credit approval.
