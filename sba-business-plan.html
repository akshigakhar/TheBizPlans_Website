SBA Business Plan: Lender Guide and Writing Service

A documented financing request, connected financial projections and a business case a lender can assess.

An SBA business plan connects a financing request to a credible operating model and a clear explanation of repayment. The document should help a lender understand the business, evaluate its assumptions and trace the requested money into the forecast. A polished narrative alone cannot do that job: the market evidence, management experience, project costs and financial schedules need to agree.

The Biz Plans prepares business plans and financial models for lender review. Founder Atul Jagga is a CPA (Ontario) with 20+ years in corporate finance and consulting. This guide explains the planning structure published by the U.S. Small Business Administration, the financial package we prepare, and the distinction between a U.S. SBA request and a Canadian financing file.

SBA programs and the lender's role

SBA describes 7(a) as its primary business loan program. Its 7(a) loans page explains that the program provides guarantees to lenders and that borrowers apply directly through a lender. SBA also says application contents vary with the loan size and processing method, with the lender identifying the documents needed for the applicant's circumstances. These are program facts checked against SBA on October 26, 2026.

This matters when someone asks for an “SBA template.” SBA's general business-planning outline is a useful foundation, but it is not a substitute for the receiving lender's checklist. Before drafting, establish who will read the file, what transaction it describes, and which supporting schedules the lender requests. A startup, an established operator and a business acquisition can need quite different evidence even when their plans share the same headings.

The other programs should not be treated as interchangeable versions of 7(a). SBA's Microloans page describes lending through designated nonprofit intermediaries, which make the credit decisions and set loan terms. SBA's 504 loans page describes long-term, fixed-rate financing for major fixed assets and the role of Certified Development Companies working with a senior lender. Both descriptions were checked October 26, 2026. This guide does not reproduce program amounts, interest rates, fees, maturity periods or eligibility tests.

A useful first planning conversation therefore concerns the project and its documentation. Identify the operating business, the proposed investment, the available historical records and the requested financing route. Ask the lender to confirm the current application checklist. The business plan can then be prepared around a real transaction rather than a generic description of business ownership.

What an SBA loan business plan should contain

SBA's current Plan your business guidance presents the traditional business-plan structure and says lenders commonly request that detailed format. The nine components below were checked against SBA's official Write your business plan guide on legacy.sba.gov, which explicitly includes the appendix. Both official sources were checked October 26, 2026. SBA presents these as common sections, with flexibility to suit the business; they are not a universal loan-application checklist. The practical drafting suggestions below explain how we use that structure.

Executive summary

Open with the business, the customer problem, the management team and the purpose of the financing. A lender should be able to identify the request without searching through a long origin story. Write this section after the financial model is stable so the amount, timing and operating milestones match the detailed schedules. Clearly distinguish established results from planned activity.

Company description

Explain what the company does, where it operates and whom it serves. Describe the stage of development and the reason for the proposed investment. A business expanding an existing operation should explain what changes after financing: capacity, facilities, service coverage or another specific constraint. Avoid broad claims about being the best provider unless the supporting evidence is available.

Market analysis

Define the reachable customer base and explain the competitive alternatives those customers already have. A national industry forecast does not establish demand for a particular local operation. Use dated research, a defined geographic area and relevant competitor observations. Connect the evidence to the sales assumptions, including the route by which potential customers become paying customers.

Organization and management

Show ownership, responsibilities and the experience relevant to executing the plan. Identify the people responsible for sales, operations and financial control. Explain important vacancies and the budget for filling them. An organization chart is helpful when it clarifies accountability; it does not replace an explanation of how the team will handle the proposed scale of activity.

Service or product line

Describe what customers buy, how it is delivered and what determines its cost. Explain the practical dependencies: supplier availability, production capacity, qualified staff, equipment or permissions. Where the offering is still being developed, state the remaining work and its funding implications. The reader should understand how the product description translates into revenue and direct costs.

Marketing and sales

Explain the channels used to reach customers, the sales process and how repeat business is developed. Match marketing spending to the forecast rather than assuming sales grow independently of effort. For a business with a long sales cycle, show the time between initial contact, delivery, invoicing and collection. That timing can materially affect working-capital needs.

Funding request

Set out the requested financing and a schedule of how the money will be used. Separate equipment, installation, premises costs, opening inventory and operating cash where relevant. Identify other proposed funding sources and distinguish committed contributions from assumptions. Use quotations and project documents to support costs, while leaving financing terms subject to the lender's confirmation.

Financial projections

Explain the model in words as well as numbers. Revenue should follow identifiable drivers, and expenses should reflect the operating plan. Link the funding request to the balance sheet, cash flow and debt schedule. Disclose assumptions and uncertainties so a reviewer can see which changes would have the largest effect on cash available for repayment.

Appendix

Organize the evidence behind the narrative, including relevant resumes, quotations, contracts, permits and other requested documents. Reference each attachment where it matters in the plan. Keep sensitive personal records in the lender's designated secure channel rather than embedding them in every circulated copy. An indexed appendix makes it easier to check a claim without interrupting the main story.

The financial package: three-year pro-forma projections

Our core financial package covers three years of pro-forma income statements, balance sheets and cash-flow projections, supported by assumptions and a funding schedule. This describes our preparation scope, not a claim that SBA universally requires exactly three years. SBA's current business-planning guidance recommends a five-year prospective outlook and more detailed first-year projections, quarterly or monthly. It also discusses historical statements for established businesses. Checked October 26, 2026.

We confirm the horizon and level of detail against the lender's instructions and extend the model when the file calls for five years. For an established business, historical results provide a starting point for explaining changes in margins, staffing and working capital. For a startup, the assumptions schedule has to carry more of the explanation because operating history is unavailable.

The income statement shows the relationship between sales, costs and profit. The cash-flow forecast tracks when money actually moves. The balance sheet connects assets, liabilities and owner investment at each reporting date. These statements need to reconcile: equipment purchases, financing proceeds, loan principal payments and unpaid customer invoices cannot simply disappear between schedules.

We build sales from operating drivers such as units sold, customer visits, billable capacity or recurring accounts. We then test whether the staffing, premises and equipment budgets can support that activity. Growth needs an operational explanation. If revenue rises because capacity expands, the forecast should include the associated cost and timing of that expansion.

Working capital deserves a separate explanation. A business can report profit while cash is tied up in inventory or unpaid invoices. The model should show collection assumptions, supplier payments, inventory purchases and the timing of operating expenses. Where a seasonal business has a quiet period, an annual total alone can hide the cash pressure that appears in individual months.

A downside scenario tests the plan's sensitivity to a slower opening, delayed collections or lower demand. The purpose is to identify the point at which the business needs to reduce spending, change timing or obtain another confirmed funding source. Scenarios are planning tools, not predictions. Their assumptions should be visible and consistently applied across all statements.

Break-even analysis and debt-service coverage

Break-even analysis asks how much activity is needed to cover the costs included in the calculation. A basic operating model divides fixed operating costs by the contribution margin per unit. State precisely which costs are included: an operating break-even calculation can leave out loan principal payments and therefore does not automatically establish that cash is sufficient for debt service.

Illustrative example only: suppose a service sells for an illustrative USD $100 per appointment and incurs illustrative variable costs of USD $40. Its illustrative contribution is USD $60 per appointment. With illustrative fixed operating costs of USD $12,000 per month, the illustrative operating break-even volume is 200 appointments per month: USD $12,000 divided by USD $60. These invented figures demonstrate the calculation; they are not an industry benchmark or financing recommendation.

Compare the resulting activity level with practical capacity. Can the business serve that many customers with the planned staffing and opening hours? Does the sales approach support that demand? If the answer depends on running at full capacity immediately, the plan should make that risk clear rather than presenting the break-even result as reassurance.

Debt-service coverage asks a different question: how does cash available for debt service compare with scheduled debt payments? Conceptually, debt-service coverage ratio equals cash available for debt service divided by debt service over the same period. Lenders calculate their own ratio using their definitions, adjustments and treatment of obligations. This explanation does not prescribe an SBA threshold or a lender's underwriting method.

Illustrative example only: using illustrative annual cash available for debt service of USD $150,000 and illustrative annual principal-and-interest payments of USD $120,000 produces illustrative coverage of 1.25 times. If illustrative available cash falls to USD $108,000 while the illustrative payments remain USD $120,000, illustrative coverage falls to 0.90 times. These figures are not approval standards. The lender determines the applicable calculation and whether the overall file supports lending.

The practical value lies in tracing the inputs. State whether the numerator reflects working-capital movements and which obligations enter the denominator. Keep forecast periods consistent. Where proposed borrowing terms are not confirmed, label the debt schedule assumptions and update them when the lender supplies actual terms. Precision in presentation cannot compensate for uncertain inputs.

Personal financial statements and collateral documents

A personal financial statement describes an individual's assets, liabilities and related financial position; it is distinct from the business's projected statements. SBA's official Form 413: Personal Financial Statement page says the form is used to assess repayment ability and creditworthiness for programs including 7(a) and 504. That purpose was checked October 26, 2026. The lender should identify the current form and who must complete it for the particular application.

A collateral schedule describes assets proposed as security and the evidence supporting their description and value. It may organize ownership information, asset details, existing obligations and relevant valuation documents. Listing an asset does not establish its acceptable lending value or confirm that it meets a program's requirements. The lender determines what documentation and evaluation are needed.

Keep personal and business records clearly identified. A plan can refer to documents supplied separately without reproducing account numbers or personal identifiers in its narrative. Use the receiving institution's secure submission process. These descriptions explain document types; they do not advise an individual how to pledge assets or structure personal obligations.

SBA plan versus CSBFP plan for cross-border founders

A founder operating on both sides of the border needs a separate financing case for each operating business and lender. The transferable discipline is the same: identify the borrower, document the project, explain assumptions and show repayment capacity. The governing program rules remain separate. This comparison is qualitative; Canadian program details belong in our Canada Small Business Financing Program guide.

Common reasons loan plans fall short

The following are practical document weaknesses we look for during preparation and review. They are not a ranked list of SBA rejection reasons, and no frequency or approval statistics are implied.

Thin market analysis

A plan can contain extensive industry commentary yet say little about its reachable customers. Improve it by defining the trading area, the buyer, competing alternatives and the evidence for demand. Explain the limitations of the research. A documented customer conversation is useful evidence of interest, but it should not be described as a binding order unless it actually is one.

Unsupported projections

Forecasts become difficult to evaluate when growth is entered as an arbitrary percentage. Show the operational drivers and attach support where available. Distinguish contracted sales from prospects and capacity from demand. Reconcile the narrative with the model so planned hiring, equipment purchases and marketing activity occur in the periods used by the forecast.

Missing funding-request detail

A round total without a cost schedule leaves important questions unanswered. Identify each major use, when it must be paid and the source covering it. Explain quotation dates and assumptions about unconfirmed costs. Include the cash needed between making an investment and collecting revenue so the request reflects the operating cycle rather than just the purchase price.

Inconsistent documents

Check that ownership, project dates, investment amounts and forecast versions match across the package. A credible plan should be easy to reconcile to the submitted records. Maintain a document index and resolve differences openly. If a figure is provisional, label it and state what evidence will replace it instead of quietly presenting it as established fact.

How The Biz Plans prepares the lender file

We begin with a scope discussion covering the transaction, lender checklist, available records and required delivery format. The agreed scope identifies the narrative, forecast horizon, supporting schedules and revision process. This helps the owner understand what will be prepared and which inputs remain their responsibility.

Next comes evidence gathering. We organize financial history, management information, project quotations and market research. We flag missing inputs before they become unsupported claims. The financial model develops alongside the narrative so changes to the operating plan flow into the projections and funding request.

Review focuses on consistency and traceability. We compare the summary with the detailed request, test the model's internal relationships and confirm that important assumptions are explained. The owner reviews business-specific facts. The final package is organized for the lender's review, with follow-up revisions handled within the agreed scope. The lender retains responsibility for underwriting and its decision.

SBA business plan FAQs

Does the SBA require a business plan for a 7(a) loan?

The lender requests the business plan as part of its application package. SBA’s 7(a) loans page says application contents depend on loan size and the lender’s processing method, and the lender determines the documents needed for the applicant’s circumstances. SBA’s planning guide says lenders commonly request a traditional business plan. Confirm the receiving lender’s checklist; this is not a claim that SBA mandates one identical plan for every 7(a) application.

Source: SBA, 7(a) loans; SBA planning guidance. Checked October 26, 2026.

What financial projections does an SBA business plan need?

Prepare linked income statements, balance sheets and cash-flow forecasts with explained assumptions. SBA’s general business-planning guidance recommends a five-year outlook, with quarterly or monthly detail in the first year. Our core package covers three years and is extended when the lender requests five years or another horizon. Confirm historical-statement and supporting-schedule requirements with the lender.

Source: SBA, Plan your business. Checked October 26, 2026.

How long should an SBA business plan be?

Use the length needed to explain the business and substantiate the request. SBA describes traditional plans as detailed and potentially dozens of pages long, rather than prescribing one universal page count. Keep the main narrative focused and place supporting evidence in an indexed appendix. The lender’s requested content matters more than reaching a target number of pages.

Source: SBA, Plan your business. Checked October 26, 2026.

SBA plan versus a regular business plan: what is actually different?

The underlying business description can be similar. A loan-focused plan gives particular attention to the funding request, project costs, cash-flow timing, proposed debt payments and evidence supporting repayment assumptions. It must also match the receiving lender’s application checklist. The SBA planning outline provides a structure; it does not replace program review or the lender’s underwriting.

Can I use a free generator or template and still apply?

Yes. A generator or template can organize a first draft, but using one does not establish that the application is complete. Check business facts, research, financial assumptions and supporting documents against the lender’s instructions. A self-built plan is a starting draft; lender files are typically professionally reviewed. The lender decides whether the submitted package is sufficient.
