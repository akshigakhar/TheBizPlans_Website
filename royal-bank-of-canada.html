RBC Business Loans and Financing in Canada

Information and planning support for an RBC small business financing application

Reviewed August 2026. Financing products, eligibility and application processes can change. Verify current details directly with RBC. The Biz Plans is an independent business-plan consulting firm and is not affiliated with or endorsed by RBC.

RBC offers business financing for needs such as working capital, equipment, property and growth. The suitable structure depends on the amount, intended use, cash-flow cycle and circumstances of the business.

Start with the current RBC business loans and financing overview, then confirm available products, terms and application documents directly with RBC.

RBC Business Financing Options

Business borrowing can serve different purposes. A term facility may finance a defined asset or project, while revolving credit may help bridge the timing between paying expenses and collecting customer receipts. RBC also provides business banking information about financing for property, vehicles and equipment.

Product availability, credit limits, rates, security and repayment terms are determined by RBC. Review RBC's official information and speak with the bank about which option may fit the proposed use of funds.

RBC Small Business Loans

An RBC small business loan may be considered when a business has a defined funding need and repayment period. Application materials should connect the amount requested to quotations, purchase costs or another documented use and explain how the business expects to make the proposed payments.

For eligible transactions, the federal Canada Small Business Financing Program (CSBFP) may also be relevant. Program eligibility does not mean automatic financing approval; confirm RBC's participation, the current program rules and the bank's credit requirements directly.

RBC Business Lines of Credit

A business line of credit is generally designed for recurring or short-term working-capital needs rather than a one-time long-lived purchase. The supporting forecast should show the cash-conversion cycle, expected draws and repayments, seasonal peaks and maximum borrowing need—not only annual profit.

See RBC's current business line-of-credit information and confirm the available facilities and terms with the bank.

Preparing for an RBC Business Financing Application

The documents requested depend on the product, borrower and transaction. Useful preparation may include ownership and identification records, historical and interim financial statements, tax records, existing debt details, quotations or purchase agreements, management backgrounds and forward-looking projections.

This is general preparation guidance, not a statement of RBC's requirements. Ask RBC which forms, supporting documents, security and guarantees apply to your application.

Business Plan for RBC Business Financing

A financing-focused RBC business plan should make the request, business case and repayment logic easy to follow. Depending on the financing request, a lender may need information concerning:

  • business background, ownership and management;
  • the amount requested, purpose of financing and owner contribution;
  • historical financial results and existing or proposed debt;
  • the market opportunity, customers and competitors;
  • revenue assumptions and operating expenses;
  • cash-flow projections; and
  • repayment capacity under realistic assumptions.

These are general lender and business-plan considerations, not items RBC necessarily requires in every application. For a more detailed preparation framework, read the Canadian Bank Loan Business Plan Guide.

Financial Projections for RBC Financing

Financing projections should translate the written strategy into a connected model. The logic may move from revenue to gross margin, operating expenses, EBITDA or profit, working capital, capital expenditures, loan payments and the resulting cash position.

Assumptions should reconcile with the written plan, sources and uses of funds, proposed financing and balance sheet. Sensitivity analysis can also show the impact of slower sales, delayed collections or higher costs. Learn more about how banks evaluate business plans and financial projections.

Acquisition Financing and Business Purchase Plans

A business acquisition can require a particularly detailed financing case. An acquisition plan may connect the purchase price, buyer investment and financing structure to the target's historical performance, normalized earnings, post-acquisition assumptions and expected debt servicing.

The model should distinguish historical results from buyer assumptions and explain integration costs, working-capital needs and downside scenarios. Our Bank Loan Business Plan service can incorporate the acquisition narrative and financing projections into one lender-ready package.

What Lenders Look for in a Business Plan

Lenders generally assess more than the plan's presentation. They may consider the owners' experience and investment, the reliability of historical results, the evidence behind forecast assumptions, available security, existing obligations and the cash available for repayment.

A clear plan should identify important risks, explain reasonable responses and use consistent figures throughout. See the full guide to how banks evaluate business plans.

Helpful answers

RBC Business Financing FAQs

Practical answers to help you evaluate this topic and prepare your next step.

What should a business plan for RBC financing include?

Include the amount requested, an itemized use of funds, owner contribution, market evidence, operating plan and integrated financial projections. The assumptions should demonstrate how the business expects to generate enough cash to meet its obligations.

How many years of financial projections are normally useful?

A monthly cash-flow view for the near term and annual projections for later years are often useful, but the appropriate period depends on the lender, program and business stage. Confirm the recipient's current requirements before submitting.

Does a strong business plan guarantee financing approval?

No. The lender or program makes its own eligibility, credit and security decisions. A well-supported plan helps reviewers understand the opportunity and risks, but it cannot guarantee approval or a particular financing amount.

What documents should support the financing request?

Depending on the application, useful records may include owner résumés, quotes, leases, historical statements, tax records, debt schedules, licenses and evidence of equity. Provide only documents relevant to the reviewer and verify current requirements directly.

How should repayment risk be addressed?

Show realistic margins, working-capital needs, debt payments and cash timing. Include sensitivities for important variables such as slower sales, lower pricing or higher costs, then explain the actions management could take.

When should the plan be reviewed before submission?

Review it after the funding structure and supporting quotes are known, and again immediately before submission. Reconcile every funding figure across the narrative, cash flow, balance sheet and use-of-funds schedule.
