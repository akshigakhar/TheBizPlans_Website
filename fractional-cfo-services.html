Fractional CFO Services

Senior finance support for your next stage of growth.

Fractional CFO services give growing Canadian businesses senior finance leadership without committing to a full-time hire. What does a fractional CFO do?

A fractional CFO translates financial information into choices an owner can act on. The starting point is the business model: how you earn revenue, when customers pay, what delivery costs, and which commitments consume cash before sales arrive. The aim is a forecast and reporting process that management can understand, challenge and use.

Cash-flow forecasting

A profitable business can still struggle to meet payroll when collections lag behind spending. We connect expected receipts, supplier payments, payroll, debt service and tax obligations so you can see where cash pressure may develop and discuss actions before committing funds.

Financial modelling

A useful model connects sales drivers, capacity, staffing and working capital rather than simply increasing last year’s revenue. We test the assumptions behind hiring, equipment purchases or expansion and show how a downside case affects liquidity; explore our financial modelling services for project-specific modelling needs.

Lender and investor reporting

A lender needs a clear repayment story, while an investor may focus on performance drivers and the use of capital. We help organize reporting around the intended reader, explain actual results against the forecast, and make significant variances visible rather than burying them in a spreadsheet.

Pricing and margin analysis

Revenue growth is less useful if every additional order absorbs cash or reduces contribution. We examine the relationship between price, delivery cost, customer mix and overhead so you can assess a discount, contract or new product with the economics in view.

Funding readiness

Before approaching a lender, reconcile the financing request with the use of funds, owner contribution and repayment capacity. A CFO can help identify gaps in the forecast and supporting records; our bank loan business plans and investor business plans address the separate written case when it is needed.

How does a fractional CFO engagement work?

The sequence is discovery, financial diagnostic and an ongoing management cadence. The scope depends on the quality of your records, the decisions ahead and the responsibilities already covered by your team. We agree the work before treating any particular report or meeting schedule as part of the engagement.

1. Discovery: define the decisions

Start with the questions keeping you occupied: a borrowing request, uncertain cash availability, a margin problem or an expansion proposal. We discuss your current bookkeeper and accountant, available financial statements, accounting systems and the information management needs. This establishes who owns each input and what a useful outcome would look like.

2. Financial diagnostic: establish a usable baseline

Review the financial statements, receivables, payables, debt commitments and operating assumptions that matter to the scope. Identify inconsistencies or missing information before using those numbers in forecasts. Where records need reconciliation or tax treatment needs clarification, coordinate with the responsible adviser instead of hiding the issue inside a model.

3. Ongoing cadence: report, forecast and decide

Agree how reporting, forecast updates and decision discussions will fit your operations. Compare actual performance with the assumptions, explain changes and assign follow-up actions. The conversation should end with a clearer decision, an owner for the next action and an understanding of what information still needs to be confirmed.

Fractional CFO vs. bookkeeper vs. full-time CFO

The roles solve different problems, and a business may need several of them at once. Titles and responsibilities vary between firms, so confirm the actual scope rather than assuming that every accountant provides forecasting or every CFO performs transaction processing.

You may not need a fractional CFO if the immediate problem is unreconciled accounts, overdue filings or basic transaction processing. Resolve those foundations with the appropriate bookkeeper or accountant first. A stable business with straightforward operations may only need a discrete modelling project or an occasional planning review. Conversely, a business needing daily executive involvement should consider whether a full-time finance leader better matches the workload.

Who needs a fractional CFO?

Fractional CFO for small business work is driven by decision complexity, not a universal revenue threshold. Consider the frequency and importance of the financial choices you face, and whether your existing team has the capacity to evaluate them.

Businesses preparing to raise or borrow

You need to connect the funding request to realistic operating assumptions, cash requirements and repayment capacity. Review our CSBFP guide and BDC financing guide where relevant. Program and lender requirements must be checked for the actual application; preparing a model does not determine eligibility or approval.

Operators outgrowing their bookkeeping reports

Your records show what happened, but do not explain whether you can afford new staff, a larger location or a major customer contract. CFO support can connect those choices to capacity, contribution and cash timing. Keep the bookkeeper’s transaction work in place so management analysis rests on reliable records.

Owners planning an exit or acquisition

A transaction raises questions about earnings quality, working capital and the cash needed after closing. Financial leadership can help organize assumptions and identify issues for legal, tax and transaction specialists. Our partner buyout planning page provides related context when ownership is changing.

Growing businesses with uneven cash cycles

Seasonality, inventory purchases or slow-paying customers can make a healthy sales pipeline difficult to finance. A forward view helps distinguish a temporary timing gap from a pricing or operating problem. Forecast Canadian obligations such as GST/HST remittances and CRA payments using the applicable treatment and dates confirmed with your accountant.

Fractional CFO Canada: frequently asked questions

How much does a fractional CFO cost in Canada?

Pricing depends on the agreed scope: the state of your records, complexity of the business, reporting needs, modelling requirements and level of ongoing involvement. The Biz Plans does not publish a standard fractional CFO rate, so a generic monthly figure would not describe your engagement responsibly. Request a free quote with your business stage, immediate decisions and current finance setup. Ask the proposal to clarify deliverables, the fee basis, applicable taxes and how additional work will be approved; confirm amounts in CAD.

How much time does a fractional CFO spend with my business?

The time commitment follows the responsibilities you agree, rather than a fixed allocation promised before discovery. A business preparing a financing case may need a different pattern of support from one reviewing operating performance. Discuss forecast updates, meetings, access between discussions and who prepares source information. Your team also needs time to supply records and act on findings. The proposal should make the working cadence and availability clear before the engagement begins, including how urgent requests are handled.

Do you provide remote fractional CFO support across Canada?

The Biz Plans is Toronto-based and serves businesses across Canada. For owners comparing virtual CFO services Canada options, remote discussions and shared financial information can support forecasting, reporting and decision reviews without assuming an on-site finance executive. Confirm the communication arrangements and any location-specific needs during discovery. Canadian tax, provincial requirements and GST/HST treatment should be addressed with the appropriate adviser for your business; they should not be assumed identical for every province, entity or transaction.

How is a fractional CFO different from my accountant?

Your accountant may focus on financial statements, tax filings and accounting advice, depending on the engagement. A fractional CFO focuses on the forward view: cash, operating assumptions, performance against plan and the financial implications of management choices. Some accountants also provide these services, so compare responsibilities rather than titles. The useful question is which decisions currently lack support. Agree how the CFO and accountant will coordinate, especially when a forecast depends on accounting adjustments or tax treatment that needs confirmation.

Do you replace my bookkeeper?

Usually, CFO support complements the bookkeeper rather than replacing transaction processing. Accurate entries, reconciliations and current receivables and payables are inputs to useful analysis. If those records are incomplete, the diagnostic should identify the gap and assign the cleanup to the appropriate person before management relies on the forecast. Define who maintains the ledger, closes the accounts, prepares reports and reviews results. That division of responsibility helps prevent duplicated work and makes it easier to resolve questions about the numbers.

How will confidential financial information be handled?

Discuss confidentiality and information-sharing arrangements before sending sensitive records. Agree which documents are necessary, who may access them, the method of exchange and any confidentiality terms required for the engagement. Do not send banking passwords or unrestricted system credentials in an initial enquiry. The site’s privacy policy provides the published starting point; ask about any additional requirements your business has. Specific access controls, retention arrangements or security commitments should be confirmed in writing rather than assumed from a general service description.

How do I request a free quote for fractional CFO services?

Use the project brief to describe your business, the decision you face and the support you already receive from a bookkeeper or accountant. Explain whether the need is financing, cash visibility, reporting, margin analysis or a transaction, and note any external deadline. Start with a summary rather than confidential attachments. The next step is to clarify the scope, available records and working arrangements so you can review a relevant proposal before committing to the engagement.
