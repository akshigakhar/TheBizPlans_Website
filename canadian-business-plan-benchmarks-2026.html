Canadian Business Plan Benchmarks 2026

A source-by-source view of financing access, business survival, startup costs and the evidence lenders examine.

The Biz Plans' Canadian Business Plan Benchmarks 2026 brings together public-source research on financing access, business survival and startup costs, alongside practitioner commentary on preparing a business plan.

This is a compilation of existing research, not a new survey or a measure of our clients' outcomes. The edition year is not the observation year: use the source periods below when citing a figure. These comparisons do not predict an individual loan or immigration decision.

Benchmark findings

Application participation and application approval describe different groups. An aggregate approval rate does not establish the odds for a startup, a particular lender or a particular funding request.

Average microbusiness launch cost$120,000BMO/Pollara via The Canadian Press

This dated poll is not a current startup budget. It has not been inflation-adjusted here. Use current supplier quotations and explain the timing of purchases, operating costs and cash receipts.

Lender requirements: qualitative planning checklist

Practitioner commentary in the existing benchmarks article emphasizes repayment capacity, use of funds, collateral and guarantees, documented assumptions, and founder experience. These are review questions, not measured approval thresholds. Confirm the documents and criteria with the actual lender.

Why the benchmarks matter

For entrepreneurs

Use the comparisons to identify questions your plan must answer. Build your own budget and cash forecast from evidence, and explain where your business differs from a broad historical comparison. Organize a first draft with the business plan generator.

For lenders

A clear plan makes the requested funds, supporting evidence and repayment assumptions easier to review together. These benchmarks provide context rather than underwriting standards. Explore the CSBFP guide, CSBFP in Numbers, BDC guide, bank-loan business plans, TD guide and RBC guide.

For immigration applicants

Use research to challenge commercial assumptions and explain the proposed operation. Canadian financing and survival statistics do not establish immigration eligibility or replace program-specific evidence. Review the immigration business plan guidance and confirm current requirements for your application.

Methodology and source limitations

This report uses desk research plus practitioner commentary, as documented in the existing benchmarks article. It is not a client-engagement dataset or an original survey. The tables reproduce selected figures already present in that article without new estimates, rounding or calculations. Source periods and publication dates differ; consult the original publications for definitions, sampling and coverage before reusing a statistic.
