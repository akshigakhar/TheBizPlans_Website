What Is an SME?

The Canadian definition and why it matters for your business.

An SME is a small and medium-sized enterprise, a business classified as small or medium according to the definition being used.

In Canada, the familiar definition uses paid employees. However, SME is a size category, not a legal structure, a certification or a promise of financing. Understanding which definition applies helps you describe your business accurately and avoid confusing a statistical label with a program's eligibility rules.

This glossary guide explains the Canadian employee bands, compares them with other approaches, and shows how to use the term in funding applications and business plans. For related financial terms, visit our business finance glossary.

How Canada defines an SME

Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED), in Key Small Business Statistics 2025, defines SMEs as enterprises with 1–499 paid employees. Its categories are:

  • Small business: 1–99 paid employees.
  • Medium-sized business: 100–499 paid employees.
  • Large business: 500 or more paid employees.

The same ISED report identifies micro-enterprises as businesses with 1–4 employees. This is a sub-band within small business, rather than a separate category outside SMEs. These are the report's published definitions, using Statistics Canada data sources; they should not be assumed to govern every Canadian survey or application.

The word “paid” matters. ISED generally excludes enterprises without paid employees from this publication. A self-employed owner working alone can operate a real business while falling outside this particular employer-business population. Similarly, size classification does not tell you whether a business is incorporated, profitable or newly established.

ISED generally measures businesses at the enterprise level. When you use its statistics, keep that unit in mind: a location or establishment is not necessarily an entire enterprise. For an application, follow the form's instructions about headcount, reporting date and related businesses rather than importing assumptions from a statistical table.

The Business Development Bank of Canada (BDC), in its Canadian small-business explainer updated January 2025, also describes SMEs using paid-employee bands. This supports using headcount when explaining the general Canadian term, while checking product-specific criteria when seeking financing.

How other definitions differ

Employee counts are useful for Canadian economic reporting, but financing rules can use revenue. International definitions also differ. The verified examples below illustrate why an SME description needs context.

The CSBFP row is a program eligibility example, not a replacement national SME definition. It also does not establish a universal BDC or bank revenue threshold. Ask the intended lender which definition applies to the specific product and how it measures revenue and business size.

Why the SME label matters

Program eligibility and lender programs

Grants, financing initiatives and lender programs may target small or medium-sized businesses, but each sets its own conditions. Geography, sector, ownership, business stage and proposed spending can matter alongside size. Start with our Ontario small business grants guide, then check the administering body's current rules before committing time or expenditure.

The Canada Small Business Financing Program illustrates the distinction. ISED's official guidance uses revenue-based eligibility and states that the financial institution makes the lending decision. Being an SME in a statistical report does not automatically qualify a business for a CSBFP loan or another lender product.

Statistics and market comparisons

A consistent size definition helps readers compare business counts and employment across sectors. In your business plan, identify the source, reference year, size category and population behind a statistic. Avoid treating a share of employer businesses as a share of all entrepreneurs, or comparing datasets with different coverage as though they measured the same thing.

Procurement and set-asides

Size can matter when assessing supplier opportunities, especially across jurisdictions. However, SME status alone does not establish eligibility for Canadian procurement set-asides. CanadaBuys guidance on the Procurement Strategy for Indigenous Business explains that these set-asides are restricted to eligible Indigenous businesses. Read the solicitation's specific requirements; do not assume the U.S. approach applies in Canada.

SMEs in the Canadian economy

ISED's Key Small Business Statistics 2025, Table 1, reports that small businesses represented 98.2% of Canadian employer businesses in December 2024, while medium-sized businesses represented 1.5%. The table names Statistics Canada's Business Register among its sources. These figures describe employer businesses, not the entire self-employed population.

This helps explain why SMEs are central to discussions of entrepreneurship, local services and business financing. Their numerical prominence does not mean every SME faces the same conditions. A local service business and an expanding manufacturer can have very different staffing, equipment and working-capital needs.

ISED also cautions that methodological changes limit comparisons with earlier editions. When using a historical figure, check comparability before presenting a growth trend. For a funding plan, national context should support the explanation of your own market, customers and operating capacity.

Frequently asked questions

Is a sole proprietorship an SME?

It can be. Sole proprietorship describes legal structure; SME describes size. Under ISED's 2025 employer-business definition, a sole proprietorship with paid employees can fall within the SME bands. An owner-only business without paid employees is generally outside that report's coverage. Program eligibility must be checked separately.

How many employees make a medium business in Canada?

ISED's Key Small Business Statistics 2025 defines a medium-sized business as having 100–499 paid employees. Confirm the definition used by the particular survey, lender or program you are dealing with.

Is SME the same as SMB?

SMB means small and medium-sized business and is often used conversationally for the same broad group. The acronym alone does not establish a threshold. In Canadian statistical writing, state the ISED definition when that is the classification you mean.

Do lenders use the SME definition?

Lenders may use SME as a customer segment, but eligibility depends on the product's rules. Revenue measures can differ from ISED's employee bands, as the CSBFP example shows. Ask the lender what information and calculation basis it requires.
