Canadian Agricultural Loans Act (CALA) Business Plan

The Canadian Agricultural Loans Act (CALA) program helps farmers and agricultural co‑operatives access credit. The federal government guarantees part of each eligible loan. This guarantee lowers the risk for participating banks and credit unions.

Official Government of Canada site:
Canadian Agricultural Loans Act (CALA) Program – Agriculture and Agri‑Food Canada

What Is the CALA Program?

CALA can guarantee up to 95% of an eligible agricultural loan. Approved banks and credit unions issue the loans.

The guarantee lowers the lender’s risk. Farmers may use eligible funds to buy land, improve buildings, or invest in farm operations.

About the CALA Program – Agriculture and Agri‑Food Canada

Who Can Apply

  • Existing farmers looking to expand, improve, or refinance their operations
  • Beginning farmers establishing new farms
  • Agricultural co‑operatives with at least 50% farmer membership

Borrowers must operate in Canada. Eligible activities may include crops, livestock, greenhouses, and aquaculture.

CALA Eligibility Requirements

What Can Be Financed

  • Purchase of land and buildings for farm operations
  • Construction, repair, or improvement of farm structures
  • Purchase or installation of equipment and machinery
  • Livestock and breeding stock acquisition
  • Farm debt consolidation (refinancing high‑interest loans for improved cash flow)

What Can Be Financed – CALA Program

Loan Limits and Terms

  • Up to $500,000 for land and building purchases or improvements
  • Up to $350,000 for all other eligible purposes

Repayment periods depend on how the funds are used. Land purchases may have terms of up to 15 years. Other assets may have terms of up to 10 years.

CALA Loan Terms and Limits

How the CALA Program Works

Farmers apply through a participating bank or credit union. The lender reviews the request. If approved, the lender registers the loan with Agriculture and Agri‑Food Canada (AAFC).

This process lets farmers work directly with their lender and benefit from the government guarantee.

How the CALA Program Works – AAFC

Why the CALA Program Matters

  • Improved access to capital through government‑backed guarantees
  • Reduced lending risk for financial institutions
  • Support for farm modernization and productivity improvements
  • Longer repayment terms aligned with agricultural cycles

These benefits can improve farm cash flow and support growth.

How to Apply for a CALA Loan

  1. Contact a lender. Confirm that the bank or credit union offers CALA loans.
  2. Prepare your documents. Gather your business plan, financial forecast, quotes, and farm records.
  3. Submit your application directly to your lender.
  4. Wait for the lender’s decision. If approved, the lender registers the loan with AAFC.

CALA can help eligible farmers and co‑operatives finance growth and upgrades. A clear business plan helps the lender assess the request. It should show realistic profits, cash flow, and repayment capacity.

Preparing a business plan for CALA financing

A CALA financing plan should translate agricultural production, seasonality and asset use into a cash-flow case that a participating lender can assess. Program eligibility and lender approval are separate questions.

Planning a financing application? Start with the facility, use of funds, deadline and records already available.

Request a Free Scope Assessment

What financing reviewers are assessing

  • Applicant and proposed asset eligibility under the current program rules
  • Farm management experience and production capacity
  • The economic contribution of land, buildings, equipment or livestock financed
  • Seasonal liquidity and capacity to service debt through the production cycle
  • Exposure to yield, commodity-price, input-cost and weather variability

Financial projections to prepare

  • Monthly or seasonal cash flow aligned with planting, production and sales cycles
  • Production-volume, yield, price and input-cost schedules
  • Capital-asset, depreciation and debt schedules
  • Sensitivity cases for lower yield, weaker price or higher feed and input costs

Our CPA-led modelling process reconciles the forecast to the written operating plan and makes the principal assumptions visible for review. The appropriate period and level of detail are confirmed during scope.

Documents to organize before applying

  • Farm financial statements, tax information and production records where available
  • Asset quotations, purchase agreements and property information
  • Inventory, herd, acreage, quota or capacity records relevant to the operation
  • Ownership, management history and evidence of borrower contribution

Common business-plan weaknesses

  • Using straight-line monthly revenue for a seasonal operation
  • Forecasting production without capacity or yield evidence
  • Ignoring replacement capital and maintenance requirements
  • Failing to explain cooperative governance or member economics where applicable

These issues do not describe every credit decision. Eligibility, credit history, security, lender policy and other underwriting considerations remain outside the control of a business-plan writer.

Frequently asked questions

CALA business plan FAQs

How is an agricultural plan different from a general small-business plan?

It normally requires more explicit production, seasonality, inventory, asset and commodity assumptions.

Can a beginning farmer use projections without historical results?

Yes, but the assumptions need stronger support from capacity, market evidence, quotations and management experience.

Can the plan include a cooperative?

Yes. The scope should also explain governance, member participation, capitalization and cash distribution.

Are you affiliated with the CALA program?

No. We provide independent planning support; the participating lender and program administrator determine eligibility and credit.

Helpful answers

Frequently asked questions about Canadian Agricultural Loans Act (CALA) Business Plan

Practical answers to help you evaluate this topic and prepare your next step.

What should a business plan for Canadian Agricultural Loans Act (CALA) Business Plan include?

Include the amount requested, an itemized use of funds, owner contribution, market evidence, operating plan and integrated financial projections. The assumptions should demonstrate how the business expects to generate enough cash to meet its obligations.

How many years of financial projections are normally useful?

A monthly cash-flow view for the near term and annual projections for later years are often useful, but the appropriate period depends on the lender, program and business stage. Confirm the recipient's current requirements before submitting.

Does a strong business plan guarantee financing approval?

No. The lender or program makes its own eligibility, credit and security decisions. A well-supported plan helps reviewers understand the opportunity and risks, but it cannot guarantee approval or a particular financing amount.

What documents should support the financing request?

Depending on the application, useful records may include owner résumés, quotes, leases, historical statements, tax records, debt schedules, licenses and evidence of equity. Provide only documents relevant to the reviewer and verify current requirements directly.

How should repayment risk be addressed?

Show realistic margins, working-capital needs, debt payments and cash timing. Include sensitivities for important variables such as slower sales, lower pricing or higher costs, then explain the actions management could take.

When should the plan be reviewed before submission?

Review it after the funding structure and supporting quotes are known, and again immediately before submission. Reconcile every funding figure across the narrative, cash flow, balance sheet and use-of-funds schedule.
