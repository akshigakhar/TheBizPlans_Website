Business Loans in Canada: A Small Business Guide

Compare the routes, prepare the evidence and connect your funding request to repayment capacity.

  1. Home
  2. Financing Resource Centre
  3. Business loans in Canada

Business loans in Canada can support an equipment purchase, a launch, an acquisition or the cash needed between paying suppliers and collecting customer invoices. The useful starting point is the business need: how much cash is required, when it is needed and how the business could repay it. A small business loan application becomes easier to assess when those answers connect to evidence, rather than a large borrowing target chosen before the project is costed.

This guide comes from The Biz Plans, a CPA-led business-plan preparation practice. It maps the borrowing options and the documents worth discussing with a lender. It does not rank lenders by price or promise financing. Use the overview to shortlist suitable facilities, then follow the linked program guides for details. The financial institution makes its own credit decision, and its written offer determines the obligations you would accept.

Official sources checked: October 26, 2026. Documentation below is a preparation checklist, not a universal lending requirement. Ask your chosen lender which items apply. Program summaries are deliberately brief; current official criteria and signed agreements govern.

Small business loan options: match the facility to the need

Separate the purpose of financing from the channel that offers it. An equipment purchase, a seasonal cash gap and the purchase of a business are different requests even when the owner wants the same total amount. The table identifies routes to investigate. Security means assets pledged to support a debt; a personal guarantee creates an additional obligation for the guarantor. Neither term should be assumed from a product name. Ask what would be registered, who would sign and what happens on default.

Bank term loans

A term loan is a route to discuss when the business has a defined investment and can explain repayment over time. A bank may seek business-asset security, guarantees or both, depending on the application. Prepare a purchase budget, historical records where available, projections and owner information. Ask for the bank's actual checklist before paying for unnecessary reports. A term facility should be assessed against the project's cash generation, including the time before the new asset or location contributes revenue.

Program routes: CSBFP and BDC

CSBFP financing is delivered and approved by participating financial institutions, so bring the business proposal and cost evidence and ask that lender to confirm required security and documents; source: ISED small-business FAQ, checked October 26, 2026.

BDC financing is another route for a project that fits a current product, with financial evidence and security or guarantee arrangements to confirm for that product; source: BDC financing FAQ, checked October 26, 2026.

Futurpreneur startup loans

Futurpreneur combines startup loan financing with mentorship for eligible founders. Its official eligibility page requires a viable business plan and cash flow and authorization for a personal credit check; its Core Startup Program page requests a complete plan and 24-month cash flow for submission. Both were checked October 26, 2026. Discuss the full eligibility criteria, loan obligations and any security questions directly rather than assuming a startup product removes the need to repay.

Microlenders and Community Futures

Community lending can be worth investigating where a local organization serves your location and business type. Security and evidence vary by organization. For a concrete local example, Community Futures Sunshine Coast's lending page, checked October 26, 2026, requests a plan, financial projections, a use-of-funds statement and a personal financial statement. That is one office's checklist, not a national promise. Ask your local organization about its service area, collateral policy, guarantees and current application process before preparing a tailored submission.

Equipment financing

Equipment financing connects a request to an identifiable asset, such as machinery or a business vehicle. The financed asset may form part of the security, but ask whether additional assets or guarantees are involved. Prepare a current quotation and explain delivery, installation, maintenance, expected usage and the operating benefit. If considering a lease alongside a loan, compare ownership, residual obligations and end-of-contract options. The plan should show the entire project cost, including the cash needed while installation interrupts production, rather than only the supplier's headline price.

Lines of credit

A line of credit is a route to discuss for a cash gap that arises and reverses during normal trading. Prepare a forecast showing customer collection timing, supplier payments and seasonal inventory. A lender may ask for receivable details, inventory records, financial statements and security over business assets; the offer determines the actual conditions. Ask whether access depends on eligible assets, ongoing reporting or review. A revolving facility does not itself fix persistent operating losses, so explain the business event that allows each draw to be repaid.

Merchant and online lenders

Online is a delivery channel, not a single borrowing structure. A provider may offer a loan or another arrangement tied to sales receipts. Read the contract to identify the legal product, repayment obligation, security, guarantees and withdrawal schedule. Have bank and sales records ready if requested, and ask for total cash repayment and all fees in writing. Test the proposed deductions during a weaker sales period. Do not infer that a quick application means a suitable commitment, a particular price or an unsecured obligation.

What lenders ask for: build one consistent document package

There is no document list that every lender uses for every application. The following is a practical preparation framework. As an official starting point, BDC's guide to getting a business loan in Canada, checked October 26, 2026, discusses financial records, a business plan and supporting transaction documents. Confirm the scope, forecast period and format with the lender. A modest existing-client request may use a different process from a startup or acquisition.

Business plan and supporting evidence

The plan explains what the business sells, who buys, how delivery works and why the proposed spending makes commercial sense. Connect the sales forecast to evidence such as customer conversations, signed contracts where available, pricing research and realistic operating capacity. Distinguish an expression of interest from committed revenue. Include management experience, implementation responsibilities and material risks. A professional narrative cannot turn an unsupported sales estimate into evidence, so leave a clear trail from the claim to its source and explain important gaps openly.

Our Canadian Business Plan Benchmarks 2026 lender checklist highlights repayment capacity, uses of funds, collateral, assumption quality and founder credibility as practitioner commentary. It does not establish a universal plan requirement or an applicant's approval probability. Use those themes to check whether your evidence tells a consistent story. For help connecting research, narrative and financial schedules, explore our business-plan writing services and bring the particular lender's instructions to the scoping discussion.

Projections and historical financial records

Prepare projections that link revenue, operating costs, investment, working capital and debt payments. Explain the assumptions in plain language and show monthly cash timing around the launch or project. An operating business should gather the historical statements, interim results, tax information and bank records the lender requests. Reconcile unusual movements before submission. If the forecast assumes better margins than recent results, explain the operational change behind that improvement instead of quietly replacing actual performance with a more attractive future.

Personal financial statement and credit history

A personal financial statement records the owner's assets, liabilities, income and commitments when requested. Keep it separate from the company's balance sheet, and explain which personal funds are actually available for investment. A property value is not the same as cash available for a deposit. Review your credit records for errors and prepare a factual explanation of relevant issues. Ask how the institution obtains consent for personal and business credit checks, and which owners or guarantors are included in its review.

Collateral, down payment and transaction records

Where relevant, assemble asset information, existing loan balances, quotations, purchase agreements, leases and evidence of the proposed owner contribution. Tell the lender if an asset is already pledged elsewhere. Confirm whether a valuation or other third-party report is necessary before commissioning it. An owner contribution must appear in both the sources-and-uses schedule and the cash forecast at the correct time. If the contribution itself is borrowed, disclose that obligation so the repayment analysis includes it rather than treating it as unencumbered savings.

Use a simple file index with dates and version names. Match the legal business name and requested amount across the application, plan and forecast. Retain the exact submitted version and log follow-up questions. Consistency helps a reviewer trace the request; it also helps the owner discover an unfunded cost before making a commitment. Send sensitive financial documents through the lender's approved channel and ask who needs access rather than distributing a complete personal file to every possible provider.

Frame loan size, use of funds and debt-service capacity

Start with a funding request that names the purpose, amount, proposed facility and timing. Build a uses schedule from the work required: assets, installation, deposits and operating cash during the transition. Then identify sources such as owner cash, available business cash and requested financing. Separate confirmed sources from applications still awaiting a decision. The request should follow the project's funding gap and repayment capacity; a program ceiling does not tell you what your business should borrow.

If BDC is on your funding shortlist, read what the Business Development Bank of Canada does to understand its role before preparing a financing request.

Structure follows cash timing. Discuss a term facility for an investment whose benefits arrive over time and a revolving facility for a temporary trading cycle. Explain why the proposed repayment schedule fits the business's receipts, and ask what happens if the project starts late. A forecast should include existing debt alongside the new obligation, plus tax payments, owner withdrawals and necessary replacement spending. Annual profit alone cannot show whether cash is available on each payment date.

Worked example: an illustrative equipment project

Illustrative only. Every amount and calculation in this example is hypothetical, in Canadian dollars. These are not lending terms, required contributions, quotations, program limits or approval thresholds.

Illustrative: a small operating company budgets CAD $80,000 for installed equipment, CAD $10,000 for training and transition costs, and CAD $30,000 for additional operating liquidity. Its illustrative total use of funds is CAD $120,000. The owner proposes CAD $40,000 of cash and requests an illustrative CAD $80,000 term loan. The sources therefore equal the uses. The lender would still need to assess the project, eligible costs if applicable, contribution, security and repayment case.

Assume illustrative total annual principal and interest payments of CAD $24,000 across existing and proposed debt. The illustrative coverage calculation is $36,000 ÷ $24,000 = 1.50 times, leaving an illustrative CAD $12,000 before any cash items omitted from that definition. The assumed payment is a modelling input, not a quoted loan repayment or implied interest rate.

Illustrative downside: if cash available falls to CAD $21,000 while the assumed payments remain CAD $24,000, coverage becomes 0.875 times and the annual shortfall is CAD $3,000. This exposes a question to resolve through the project budget, spending schedule or financing discussion. Neither ratio is an approval test. Lenders define available cash and acceptable coverage differently; ask for their definition and include all material cash obligations in the actual model.

The next check is monthly timing. A business could show positive annual coverage while supplier deposits and delayed collections create an early cash shortage. Test a later delivery, slower sales and an interruption in production. Explain which costs can be postponed and where any additional cash would come from. Eligibility reality for small business loans in Canada

Program eligibility and lender approval answer different questions. Eligibility concerns whether the applicant and project fit a defined route. A credit decision concerns whether the lender is prepared to accept the risk on the proposed terms. An owner can pass an initial eligibility screen and still be asked for more evidence, a different structure or a smaller request. Treat an early conversation as fact finding and keep commitments conditional on financing where appropriate.

Startups and operating businesses present different evidence

A startup cannot provide operating history it does not have. It can provide founder experience, documented project costs, market evidence, an implementation schedule and a forecast with an explicit sales ramp. An operating business can compare forecasts with actual sales, margins and cash collection. If recent performance is weak, explain the cause and the concrete changes underway. Time in business alone does not explain viability, and a newly incorporated company should not be described as having the owner's entire employment history as business revenue.

For a no-revenue startup, ISED's small-business FAQ includes eligible startups, while Futurpreneur's Core Startup Program describes a pre-startup route beyond the idea or research stage; both checked October 26, 2026. These are routes to investigate, not automatic eligibility for every new venture. The linked CSBFP guide explains the lender-led route without repeating its program rules here.

Owner credit and business history still matter

There is no single published credit-score threshold covering most programs and lenders. Product-specific criteria can exist; do not apply one lender's rule to the entire market. Ask which product is being assessed, what business history it expects and which owners' credit information it reviews. BDC's financing FAQ, checked October 26, 2026, describes a broader review of business potential, cash flow and management alongside credit. That does not mean a particular BDC product has no minimum criteria.

Before approaching a lender, identify issues that could affect the file: tax arrears, overdue accounts, disputed credit entries, an incomplete owner contribution or a lease that cannot accommodate the intended activity. Prepare accurate records and a practical response. Avoid presenting future sales as a solution to every gap. If you are still organizing the venture itself, our Ontario business startup guide connects validation, registration, permits and launch planning; businesses elsewhere should use their province's official registration and permit sources.

Grants versus loans: build a complete funding mix

Not all business funding is debt. A grant or non-repayable contribution may support a qualifying activity, while a tax credit or wage subsidy has its own timing and conditions. A loan creates a repayment obligation under its agreement. Confirm the funding type before treating an award as cash available to spend. Our small business grants in Ontario pillar separates these routes and links official administrators. Its geographic scope is Ontario; it is not a list of grants available to every Canadian business.

A project may involve owner cash, a loan and program assistance, but combining them depends on the agreements. Ask each administrator about assistance stacking, eligible costs and disclosure of other funding. Show reimbursements when cash is expected, rather than when the application is submitted. Include a case where assistance is delayed or declined. Never use the same proposed award twice in the sources schedule, and ask your accountant how assistance and recoverable taxes should be reflected in the actual projections.

Prepare a loan request you can explain

Before the first lender meeting, prepare a short request summary: what you want to finance, why it matters, the proposed amount, owner contribution and timing. Attach a preliminary cost schedule and list the evidence already available. Ask whether the product suits the purpose and business stage, which documents are needed and what conditions could delay funding. Record the answer and update the plan accordingly. This conversation helps define the work before you commission a detailed package.

When comparing written offers, examine the full commitment: total payments, fees, security, guarantees, reporting, covenants, early repayment provisions and access to undrawn funds. Ask for clarification of anything you cannot explain in your own words. Update the cash forecast using the actual proposed terms before deciding. A prepared plan supports that decision as well as the application, because the owner needs to understand the obligations long after the lender has finished reviewing the file.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get a small business loan in Canada?

Define the project and funding gap, shortlist a facility that fits, and ask the lender for its current checklist. Prepare the requested business and owner records, a supported use-of-funds budget and cash-flow projections. Submit consistent information and answer follow-up questions. If an offer is made, review the payments, security, guarantees and other conditions before accepting. Eligibility and a well-prepared plan do not guarantee approval.

If your funding mix includes a grant, review how to apply for small business grants in Ontario before preparing the project budget and supporting evidence.

What credit score do I need for a business loan?

There is no single published credit-score threshold covering most Canadian programs and lenders. Some products publish specific criteria, while others assess the application individually. Ask the chosen lender about the specific product and whose credit it will review. Credit history, existing obligations, cash flow and the business case can all be relevant; a score alone is not a reliable approval prediction.

Can a startup with no revenue get a loan?

A no-revenue startup can investigate startup-compatible routes, subject to the full criteria and a credit decision. ISED includes eligible startups in its CSBFP small-business FAQ, and Futurpreneur describes a pre-startup route beyond the idea or research stage. Both official pages were checked October 26, 2026 and are linked in the eligibility section. Prepare founder evidence, project costs, a plan and supported cash-flow assumptions; startup status is not an approval promise.

How much can I borrow for my business?

Start with documented project costs, available contributions and the resulting funding gap, then test what repayments the business could support. A lender decides the amount it will offer. This guide does not reproduce program ceilings: see the linked CSBFP and BDC guides and confirm current official limits and any cost-category restrictions with the provider. A published maximum is not an entitlement or a recommended borrowing target.

Do I need a business plan to get a business loan?

Ask the lender. A startup, acquisition or major change may need a detailed plan, while some other applications use a shorter process. The useful underlying evidence remains the business model, management experience, supported costs and repayment forecast. Futurpreneur explicitly asks for a viable business plan and cash flow on its official eligibility page, checked October 26, 2026. Follow the checklist for the particular application rather than assuming every lender uses the same format.

Can I combine grants and business loans?

Possibly, if the loan and assistance agreements permit the combination. Disclose all proposed sources and confirm stacking rules, eligible costs and payment timing with each administrator. Keep unapproved support separate from confirmed funding, avoid counting the same source twice and model delayed reimbursement. The linked Ontario grants guide helps distinguish grants, loans and tax incentives; an accountant can help reflect assistance correctly in the financial model.
