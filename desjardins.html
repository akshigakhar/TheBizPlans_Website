Desjardins is the largest financial co-operative in North America and a leading provider of business financing in Québec and Ontario. With more than 7.5 million members and clients, Desjardins combines the stability of a major financial institution with a community-driven approach to banking. Its business loans and credit solutions are tailored to help entrepreneurs, professionals, and co-operatives finance start-ups, manage growth, and invest in modernization.

What Is a Desjardins Business Loan?

Desjardins offers flexible financing options for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), agricultural producers, and co-operatives. Its business loans are structured around your goals — whether you need funds to buy equipment, acquire property, expand operations, or strengthen working capital. Each application is evaluated on your project’s viability, repayment capacity, and contribution to your region’s economic development.

Types of Desjardins Business Financing

  1. Business Loan
    Provides long- or short-term financing to cover equipment purchases, expansion, or modernization projects. Features competitive fixed or variable rates and repayment schedules aligned with cash flow.
    👉 Business Loan Overview
  2. Line of Credit
    Offers continuous access to funds to manage day-to-day expenses, pay suppliers, or handle seasonal cash-flow variations. Interest is payable only on the funds used.
    👉 Business Line of Credit
  3. Equipment Financing and Leasing
    Enables entrepreneurs to purchase or lease new or used equipment through flexible and tax-efficient options. Ideal for manufacturers, agricultural producers, and service businesses investing in modernization.
  4. Commercial Real Estate Financing
    Long-term funding for purchasing, constructing, or renovating commercial and industrial properties. Repayment schedules are adapted to revenue streams and project timelines.
    👉 Commercial Real Estate Financing
  5. Agricultural and Agri-Food Loans
    Custom-designed credit solutions for farmers, agri-producers, and food processors through Desjardins Agricultural Financing. Includes financing for land, equipment, and operational needs.
    👉 Agricultural Financing
  6. Co-operative and Social Enterprise Financing
    As a co-operative institution, Desjardins actively supports financing for co-ops, social enterprises, and non-profits that contribute to local economies.
  7. Government and Partner Programs
    Desjardins collaborates with programs such as:
    • Canada Small Business Financing Program (CSBFP)
    • Investissement Québec and regional development funds
    • Futurpreneur Canada for young entrepreneurs
    👉 Government and Partner Programs – Desjardins

Preparing a business plan for Desjardins financing

A Desjardins plan should reflect the borrower’s actual structure. A conventional SME, cooperative and agricultural business have different governance, cash-flow and capital requirements.

Planning a financing application? Start with the facility, use of funds, deadline and records already available.

Request a Free Scope Assessment

What financing reviewers are assessing

  • Business or cooperative ownership and governance
  • Management capacity and member responsibilities where applicable
  • Historical performance, borrower or member capital and repayment
  • Agricultural seasonality or SME working-capital needs
  • The economics of the financed equipment, property or expansion

Financial projections to prepare

  • Operating cash flow matched to the business cycle
  • Member, patronage or cooperative schedules where relevant
  • Agricultural production and seasonal schedules where relevant
  • Capital investment, debt service and sensitivity analysis

Our CPA-led modelling process reconciles the forecast to the written operating plan and makes the principal assumptions visible for review. The appropriate period and level of detail are confirmed during scope.

Documents to organize before applying

  • Historical statements and current results
  • Articles, bylaws, board approvals or member information for cooperatives
  • Agricultural production records or project quotations where relevant
  • Ownership, management and contribution evidence

Common business-plan weaknesses

  • Using a standard corporation narrative for a cooperative structure
  • Leaving member capitalization or governance decisions unexplained
  • Ignoring agricultural seasonality in annual averages
  • Failing to quantify how an expansion changes capacity and cash flow

These issues do not describe every credit decision. Eligibility, credit history, security, lender policy and other underwriting considerations remain outside the control of a business-plan writer.

Desjardins business plan FAQs

How does a cooperative plan differ?

It should explain governance, member participation, capitalization, economic benefits and decision authority in addition to commercial viability.

Can the plan cover an agricultural operation?

Yes. Production, seasonality, assets and commodity assumptions should be built into the model.

Can the research focus on Quebec?

Yes. Geography and language requirements should be confirmed during scope based on the business and intended reader.

Helpful answers

Frequently asked questions about Desjardins Business Loan Business Plan

Practical answers to help you evaluate this topic and prepare your next step.

What should a business plan for Desjardins Business Loan Business Plan include?

Include the amount requested, an itemized use of funds, owner contribution, market evidence, operating plan and integrated financial projections. The assumptions should demonstrate how the business expects to generate enough cash to meet its obligations.

How many years of financial projections are normally useful?

A monthly cash-flow view for the near term and annual projections for later years are often useful, but the appropriate period depends on the lender, program and business stage. Confirm the recipient's current requirements before submitting.

Does a strong business plan guarantee financing approval?

No. The lender or program makes its own eligibility, credit and security decisions. A well-supported plan helps reviewers understand the opportunity and risks, but it cannot guarantee approval or a particular financing amount.

What documents should support the financing request?

Depending on the application, useful records may include owner résumés, quotes, leases, historical statements, tax records, debt schedules, licenses and evidence of equity. Provide only documents relevant to the reviewer and verify current requirements directly.

How should repayment risk be addressed?

Show realistic margins, working-capital needs, debt payments and cash timing. Include sensitivities for important variables such as slower sales, lower pricing or higher costs, then explain the actions management could take.

When should the plan be reviewed before submission?

Review it after the funding structure and supporting quotes are known, and again immediately before submission. Reconcile every funding figure across the narrative, cash flow, balance sheet and use-of-funds schedule.
