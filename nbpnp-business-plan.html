Nova Scotia Nominee Program (NSNP) — Entrepreneur Stream

One-year performance–ready business plans with CPA-built financials and Nova Scotia market validation.

The NSNP Entrepreneur Stream offers experienced business owners and senior managers a clear pathway to settle in Nova Scotia by starting or purchasing a business and creating local employment. Applicants begin on a temporary work permit and, after at least one year of successful operation and meeting program conditions, may be nominated for permanent residence.

Official Government page:
👉 Nova Scotia Nominee Program — Entrepreneur Stream

Program Overview

  • Start a new venture or purchase and actively manage an existing Nova Scotia business.
  • Operate on a work permit first; become eligible for nomination after sustained performance (≥ 1 year).

More on NS immigration programs:
👉 Nova Scotia Immigration — Programs Overview

Eligibility & Investment Requirements

  • Age 21+ and intention to reside permanently in Nova Scotia while actively managing the business
  • Minimum net worth CAD 600,000
  • Minimum investment CAD 150,000
  • Experience: 3+ years as business owner (≥33% ownership) or 5+ years senior management
  • Language: CLB 5 (English or French)
  • Create at least one full-time job for a Canadian citizen or permanent resident
  • In-person interview after Invitation to Apply (ITA)

Eligibility details:
👉 Entrepreneur Stream — Requirements & Guide

What Provincial Officers Look For

  1. Business viability: clear fit with Nova Scotia’s market conditions
  2. Economic impact: job creation and alignment with key sectors (tourism, oceans, education, renewables)
  3. Investment credibility: lawful funds and sufficient capitalization
  4. Ownership & management: direct day-to-day involvement
  5. Community contribution: local supply chains, services, or tourism value
  6. Applicant fit: sector-relevant experience and track record
  7. Settlement intent: plan to live and integrate in Nova Scotia

Why a Strong Business Plan Is Essential

  • Nova Scotia–specific market research and competitive mapping
  • Operational plan (structure, staffing, licensing, suppliers)
  • 3+ year financials, including cash-flow forecasts
  • Job-creation plan with hiring timing and wage budgets
  • Setup timeline (registration, permits, launch, growth milestones)
  • Community engagement strategy and measurable local benefits

How to Apply — NSNP Entrepreneur Stream

  1. Submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) via the online portal.
    👉 EOI Submission — NS Immigration Portal
  2. Receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA) if your score meets the threshold.
  3. Prepare your business plan and supporting documents.
    The Biz Plans can ensure your plan meets provincial standards.
  4. Attend an in-person interview in Nova Scotia.
  5. Obtain a work-permit support letter upon approval and apply to IRCC.
  6. Operate your business in Nova Scotia for ≥ 12 months.
  7. Apply for nomination and then permanent residence through IRCC.

Frequently asked questions about Nova Scotia Nominee Program (NSNP) — Entrepreneur Stream

Practical answers to help you evaluate this topic and prepare your next step.

What should a plan for Nova Scotia Nominee Program (NSNP) — Entrepreneur Stream demonstrate?

It should present a credible Canadian business concept, the applicant's role, market evidence, implementation milestones, staffing and financially consistent projections. The exact emphasis depends on the current program and the facts of the application.

Is an immigration business plan the same as a bank plan?

No. Both require commercial credibility, but an immigration plan must also address the relevant program criteria and show how the applicant will actively implement the venture. A bank plan places greater emphasis on credit and repayment.

How is the applicant's experience reflected in the plan?

The plan should connect relevant ownership, management and industry experience to specific responsibilities and execution risks. It should not claim qualifications that are not supported by the applicant's documents.

What research makes the proposal more credible?

Use Canadian market, competitor, pricing, location and regulatory evidence that matches the actual launch geography. Sources should be current, cited and connected to forecast assumptions rather than added as general background.

Can a business plan guarantee an immigration decision?

No. Government officers and other authorized organizations make all eligibility and application decisions. A professional plan can improve clarity and consistency but cannot guarantee endorsement, nomination, a work permit or permanent residence.

Who should review the plan before filing?

The applicant should verify every factual statement and financial assumption. Regulated immigration counsel should review legal and program matters, while accounting or industry advisers may be appropriate for specialized financial and operating claims.
