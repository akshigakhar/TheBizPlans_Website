What the E-2 plan must demonstrate

A useful plan connects the commercial narrative to evidence. It explains what the company sells, how the investment is being deployed, who controls decisions and how the operating model supports the projections. Our work starts with these business questions rather than a generic template. The document should make the enterprise understandable without asking the reader to infer the relationship between an invoice, an ownership chart and a forecast.

A real and operating commercial enterprise

USCIS describes the enterprise as a real, active commercial undertaking producing goods or services for profit. The State Department distinguishes that activity from a paper organization or passive speculative holding. For a new enterprise, its guidance asks whether the business will be real and active if the visa is issued. Sources: USCIS, Nonimmigrant Services, treaty investor guidance; U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(C).

As preparers, we translate the operating concept into a launch sequence: premises or delivery arrangements, suppliers, equipment, customer acquisition, order fulfilment and responsibilities. We separate completed actions from planned actions and give every material milestone an evidence reference. A signed supplier agreement and a supplier quotation are described differently because they represent different levels of commercial readiness.

A substantial investment, at risk and committed

USCIS frames substantiality relative to the cost of purchasing or establishing the enterprise and the investor’s commitment. The State Department explains that the investment must be commercially at risk and that funds in the process of investment must be genuinely and irrevocably committed. An intention to invest or uncommitted bank funds alone does not suffice. Sources: USCIS, Nonimmigrant Services; U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(B) and U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(D).

We build an expenditure schedule that distinguishes paid amounts, documented commitments and future budgets. The legal treatment of each item belongs with the applicant’s U.S. attorney. The plan should never disguise available cash as money already invested or count the same acquisition payment in both purchase cost and working capital.

The investor’s ability to develop and direct

USCIS identifies developing and directing the enterprise as a treaty-investor requirement. The State Department’s guidance addresses ownership and managerial control. The plan therefore explains the investor’s actual authority and management duties, with the legal ownership analysis reviewed by counsel. Sources: USCIS, Nonimmigrant Services; U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(F).

An enterprise that is more than marginal

The State Department describes marginality by reference to capacity to produce more than a minimal living for the investor and family, or to make a significant economic contribution. Our analysis addresses income capacity and credible U.S. job creation together, grounded in the business model. It does not treat an illustrative headcount as a universal visa rule. Source: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(E).

Anatomy of an E-2 business plan

Our proposed structure is a preparation framework, not an agency-prescribed universal table of contents. We tailor the depth to the enterprise, transaction and evidence available, and coordinate with the attorney on the intended submission. A concise core narrative supported by clearly indexed schedules is often more useful than a long document that repeats the same claims in several sections.

Executive summary

We write the executive summary after the operating model and forecasts reconcile. It identifies the enterprise, location, customers, products, ownership, investor’s role, investment deployment and planned staffing. It summarizes the forecast using the same dates and assumptions as the financial schedules. Readers should be able to trace each significant statement to a later section rather than encountering new claims that appear only in the summary.

For an acquisition, we distinguish the seller’s historical results from the buyer’s proposed changes. For a startup, we explain what has been completed and what remains before launch. These distinctions prevent a proposed contract from becoming an existing customer or a planned location from being described as an operating facility. They also help the client identify missing records before the final review.

Company description and ownership structure

This section describes the legal entity, trading name, ownership chain, business model and responsibilities. We request formation records, ownership documents, relevant agreements and the investor’s professional background. The ownership diagram should follow the documents through any holding companies to the individual owners. The narrative identifies who approves expenditure, supervises delivery, selects suppliers and manages customer relationships.

We reconcile names, dates and percentages across the narrative and schedules, then flag inconsistencies for the client and attorney. Commercial responsibilities should be specific: overseeing procurement, managing a sales pipeline or supervising a production team tells the reader more than a broad title. The business description also explains why the investor’s experience is relevant to those responsibilities without inventing qualifications or operating history.

Investment breakdown: show categories and evidence

The investment schedule is built from records rather than a target number selected from an online example. We organize uses by category, identify the payer and payment date, cross-reference evidence and distinguish accounting classification from legal eligibility. Counsel determines which funds and arrangements qualify under the applicable rules. Our schedule makes that review easier by keeping the underlying facts visible.

A useful reconciliation follows money from its documented source through transfers to its ultimate use. We ask the client to explain unexplained movements, related-party payments and differences between invoice totals and bank debits. The forecast opening balance sheet then reflects the accounting treatment of assets, expenses and remaining cash. A payment can be part of the commercial story without being treated as a qualifying investment by the plan writer.

For conditional arrangements, provide the complete agreement to counsel. The State Department discusses circumstances in which escrow associated with a visa-conditioned business purchase may demonstrate commitment; that discussion is not a reason to assume every escrow arrangement qualifies. Source: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(B). We describe the arrangement factually and avoid telling an applicant to spend or transfer money based on this guide.

Market analysis: connect TAM, SAM and SOM to sales

The market chapter should explain the reachable customer base, competitive alternatives and commercial reasons customers would buy. We start with a defined geography and customer problem. Research should identify publication dates, geographic coverage and limitations. National industry growth may provide context, but it does not establish local demand for a particular store, professional service or manufacturing operation.

We use TAM, SAM and SOM where they help distinguish scale from accessibility. TAM is the total addressable market; SAM narrows that opportunity to what the offer and geography can serve; SOM is the realistically obtainable portion given competition and capacity. See the business plan glossary and our TAM SAM SOM guide for definitions and calculation methods.

The resulting sales forecast should work from the bottom up. For a service company, that may mean qualified enquiries, conversion, billable capacity, utilization and price. For a retailer, it may mean transactions and average basket value. We test whether the required customer volume is plausible given opening hours, staffing, delivery capacity and marketing resources. An attractive market-size chart cannot repair an operating model that has no practical way to serve the projected customers.

Competitor research should compare relevant offers, customer segments, locations and public pricing where available, with a date for each observation. The plan explains the proposed differentiation and the cost of delivering it. We also document customer concentration, seasonality and demand risks. The Canadian business-plan benchmarks study offers planning context; its Canadian findings are not evidence of U.S. local demand or E-2 eligibility.

Staffing plan and organizational chart

We build the staffing schedule around work that must be performed. Each proposed role has duties, a hiring trigger, working hours, compensation assumptions and a reporting line. The narrative explains which activities the investor will manage and which U.S. worker positions are planned as the business grows. Every job and hiring date is a projection subject to demand, funding and operating conditions, never a promise to the reader.

Illustrative organizational structure

Investor / managing director → operations lead → service or production team. The managing director also oversees sales and administration, with external accounting support shown separately. This is a fictional reporting structure, not a required E-2 organization chart.

The five-year hiring roadmap states which positions are expected at launch and which follow later capacity milestones. Payroll assumptions include start dates and relevant employer costs, with local inputs confirmed for the specific business. Part-year hires should not generate full-year output in the sales model. Contractors are identified separately from employees so the narrative does not inflate employment claims by mixing unlike categories.

U.S. job creation is discussed as evidence of the enterprise’s proposed economic contribution, connected to the State Department’s marginality guidance. The plan should explain the commercial need and affordability of the roles rather than imply that a particular number guarantees eligibility. Source: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(E). When slower sales delay hiring in a downside scenario, the narrative and payroll schedules must change together.

Five-year financial projections

Our proposed financial package includes projected income statements, cash flows and balance sheets, supported by revenue, direct-cost, payroll, investment and financing schedules. Five years is the planning horizon used in this service framework. The State Department discusses future economic capacity generally realizable within five years of commencing normal business activity in its marginality guidance; counsel should determine how that guidance applies to the case. Source: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(E).

We normally model the launch period in greater detail because cash timing is most sensitive before sales stabilize. The assumption notes explain pricing, units, collection terms, supplier terms, rent, payroll and capital spending. Historical financials, when available, are kept distinct from forecasts. Owner compensation and distributions are also distinguished so that profit, household receipts and available business cash are not presented as interchangeable measures.

Explain marginality through operating evidence

A marginality chapter connects the forecast to the enterprise’s capacity and economic contribution. The State Department recognizes both income capacity beyond a minimal living and significant economic contribution in its explanation. Source: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(E). We do not reduce that assessment to a single profit cell or an unsupported statement that the company will hire workers.

Instead, we explain the assumptions behind customer demand, operating capacity, owner compensation, payroll and reinvestment. The reader should see how planned work creates a need for employees and how the cash model supports their pay. When the enterprise is new, we identify the milestones on which future capacity depends and show what happens if those milestones move. A downside case should expose weaknesses rather than conceal them behind optimistic averages.

Supporting material may include commercial research, documented customer interest, supplier information and historical records where available. We distinguish evidence from management assumptions and record what remains uncertain. The attorney assesses the legal case; our role is to present an internally consistent business analysis with traceable inputs. Forecast results do not promise revenue, profit, employment or a visa decision.

Treaty-country eligibility and enterprise nationality

The State Department requires a qualifying treaty or equivalent basis for E classification and addresses nationality of both the investor and business. Its guidance determines business nationality through the individual owners; incorporation alone does not resolve the question. The investor’s qualifying nationality must align with the enterprise’s qualifying nationality, with complex or dual-nationality structures reviewed by counsel. Sources: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-4(A) and U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-4(B).

Use the official U.S. Department of State Treaty Countries list, including its classification column and country-specific footnotes. We link to the maintained list rather than reproduce it here. A country entry is a starting point for legal review, not a complete personal eligibility determination.

For preparation, we request an accurate ownership chart and the relevant nationality documents through the agreed document-sharing process. We flag missing ownership links or conflicting descriptions before drafting. The business plan should use the structure confirmed by counsel and match the formation, purchase and shareholder records supplied by the client.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to invest for an E-2?

There is no fixed statutory dollar minimum published in USCIS’s qualitative substantial-investment guidance. USCIS evaluates substantiality relative to the cost of the enterprise and financial commitment. The State Department expressly says there is no set minimum dollar figure. Ask your U.S. attorney to assess your particular business; this guide supplies no required dollar threshold.

Source: USCIS, Nonimmigrant Services; U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(D).

Does the money have to be spent before applying?

A future intention to spend is insufficient. State Department guidance requires real, irrevocable commitment and discusses certain conditional purchases with escrow. It also distinguishes uncommitted bank funds from investment. Whether a particular payment, contract or escrow arrangement qualifies needs review by your U.S. attorney before you commit funds.

Source: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(B).

How long should an E-2 business plan be, and what financials are included?

We scope length around the complexity of the enterprise, supporting evidence and attorney’s instructions. This service framework includes a five-year income statement, cash-flow forecast and balance sheet, with investment, payroll, sales assumptions and sensitivity schedules. That is our preparation approach, not a universal agency page-count rule.

Can a startup that is not yet operating qualify?

A new enterprise can be considered: the State Department asks whether it will be a real and active commercial undertaking producing goods or services if the visa is issued. Its investment guidance also says an applicant in the process of investing must be close to actual operations. A concept alone is insufficient; counsel should assess the specific facts.

Source: U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(C); U.S. Department of State, 9 FAM 402.9-6(B).

Do I need a U.S. immigration lawyer as well as a plan writer?

We recommend working with a licensed U.S. immigration attorney for legal advice and filing. The Biz Plans prepares the commercial plan and financial projections and is not a U.S. law firm. Atul Jagga’s RCIC-IRB licence is Canadian. This is our service recommendation, not a claim that retaining counsel is universally mandatory.
