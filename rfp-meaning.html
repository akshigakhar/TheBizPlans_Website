RFP Meaning: How a Small Business Responds

RFP stands for Request for Proposal: a document an organisation issues to solicit bids from suppliers for a product, service or project.

For a small business, receiving an RFP means deciding whether to compete, then showing how you would deliver the work. The buyer sets the requirements; your response explains your approach, capabilities and price. Start with the instructions rather than a sales brochure. A persuasive proposal still needs to satisfy every mandatory requirement.

This guide explains the terminology and a practical response path. For related planning terms, visit the Business Finance Glossary.

RFP vs RFQ vs RFI

These documents serve different purchasing needs. Read the actual solicitation because a buyer's terminology and rules can vary.

Common purposes of supplier requests
RequestMain purposeWhat suppliers provide
RFP: Request for ProposalCompare full solutions to a defined need.A proposal covering approach, capability, delivery and price against stated criteria.
RFQ: Request for QuotationCompare prices for specified goods or services.A price quotation against the specifications, plus required terms and compliance information.
RFI: Request for InformationGather information before deciding what or how to buy.Information about available options, supplier capabilities and possible approaches.

An RFQ is primarily price-focused once the requirement is specified; it does not mean other conditions disappear. An RFI helps the buyer explore the market and is not itself a promise of a contract. An RFP asks you to explain the solution as well as its cost.

What an RFP typically contains

  • Background: who the buyer is, the problem and the purpose of the purchase.
  • Scope: the work included, boundaries, requirements and any exclusions.
  • Deliverables: the outputs the buyer expects and how acceptance will be assessed.
  • Timeline: the buyer's question period, submission deadline and proposed delivery schedule.
  • Evaluation criteria: mandatory conditions, scored factors and any disclosed weightings.
  • Submission format: required headings, forms, attachments, file types and submission channel.

Also check contract terms, insurance or qualification requirements, confidentiality instructions and the pricing schedule. Keep the original documents together with any addenda. If something is unclear, use the designated question process instead of assuming your interpretation will be accepted.

How the RFP process runs

  1. Issue: the buyer publishes or distributes the RFP and its instructions.
  2. Questions: suppliers seek clarification through the stated channel; the buyer may publish answers or amendments.
  3. Submission: suppliers deliver their proposals in the required format before the stated closing time.
  4. Evaluation: the buyer checks compliance and assesses responses using the stated process. Clarifications, presentations or negotiations may form part of that process if provided for.
  5. Award: the buyer communicates its decision and follows its contract finalisation process.

Each opportunity has its own rules. Follow the current documents for dates, communications and award conditions. Being invited to submit, or being shortlisted, does not guarantee an award.

How a small business responds to an RFP

Read the evaluation criteria first

Identify the mandatory conditions before investing in a draft. Can you meet the required qualifications, capacity, delivery obligations and contract terms? Then review the scored criteria to understand what evidence matters. Make a response checklist mapping each requirement to its section, supporting document and responsible person. Decide whether the opportunity fits your business and whether you can deliver it profitably.

Mirror the requested structure

Use the buyer's headings, numbering and forms. Answer each question directly before adding context. Describe your method, who will do the work, how you will manage quality and how you will handle risks. Point evaluators to relevant evidence rather than expecting them to infer it from a general company brochure. Respect page limits and keep technical and price information separate when instructed.

Price transparently

Complete the pricing schedule exactly as requested. Explain the basis of your price, included work, assumptions, optional items and applicable taxes where the format permits. Reconcile totals across the proposal and forms. Check that the price covers the resources needed to deliver your commitments. Do not hide exclusions in small print or offer alternatives unless the RFP allows them.

Include a capability and company overview

Explain what your business does, who leads delivery and why your experience is relevant. Include the specific team skills, comparable assignments, resources and references requested. Use accurate, supportable statements and obtain permission before sharing confidential client information. Connect each capability to this buyer's scope. A business plan can supply useful company and operational background, but the proposal must answer the RFP itself.

For help organising company information and the financial case behind your delivery plan, explore our business plan writing services.

Fictional illustration: A small Canadian design studio receives an RFP for a website redesign. It maps accessibility, project management and relevant experience to the evaluation criteria, uses the supplied response headings, and itemises its proposed work in the pricing form. This illustrates preparation, not an actual procurement or award.

Before submitting, review every attachment, signature, filename and total. Recheck addenda and the submission instructions. Allow yourself room to resolve upload problems, and retain any submission confirmation provided by the system.

Where RFPs are posted in Canada

Start with CanadaBuys for federal tender opportunities. Provincial portals are another route; BC Bid is British Columbia's procurement portal. Check the relevant province or territory's official procurement website to find its current posting arrangements. Municipal websites may publish opportunities directly or link to their chosen tendering platform.

Search by your service, location and relevant categories, then read the full notice. Confirm where the documents, amendments and submission instructions are hosted. Registration, access and bidding steps depend on the platform and opportunity. Private organisations may also send RFPs directly to selected suppliers.

Frequently asked questions

What does RFP stand for?

RFP stands for Request for Proposal. It is a document an organisation issues to solicit supplier bids for a product, service or project.

RFP vs tender — same thing?

The terms can overlap. Tender can describe a competitive purchasing process or a supplier's bid; an RFP is a particular request for proposals. The title alone does not establish the rules or legal obligations. Read the solicitation and contract terms.

How long should an RFP response be?

As long as the criteria require, within any stated page or word limits. Follow the requested structure and include the evidence needed to answer each requirement. There is no universal response length.

Do small businesses win RFPs?

Yes, small businesses can win RFPs when they meet the requirements and their proposals succeed under the buyer's evaluation process. Business size alone does not establish eligibility or competitiveness. Choose opportunities that match your capacity, experience and ability to deliver.
