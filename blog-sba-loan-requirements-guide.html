SBA Loan Requirements: Eligibility, Documents and Steps

Prepare a clear financing request, supporting records and a repayment case for your lender.

SBA loan requirements involve two related questions: whether your business and proposed use of funds fit the program, and whether a participating lender can support the credit request. An SBA guarantee does not replace underwriting or your obligation to repay. Start by discussing the transaction with a lender before commissioning reports or finalising a financing-dependent purchase.

This guide explains the main programs, eligibility basics, documents and application steps in general terms. For help preparing the narrative and financial package, see our SBA business plan service and lender guide. Confirm the current program rules and your specific checklist with the receiving lender.

SBA loan types at a glance

7(a): flexible business financing

The 7(a) program provides guarantees to participating lenders for eligible small-business loans. Its uses can include working capital, equipment, real estate and changes of ownership, subject to program conditions. The program structure allows loans up to $5 million; that ceiling is not an indication of what a particular business can borrow. SBA 7(a) requirements include eligibility, creditworthiness and a reasonable ability to repay.

504: major fixed assets

The 504 program supports major fixed assets, such as eligible commercial property and long-lived equipment. A Certified Development Company (CDC) works with a senior lender on the financing structure. It is not a general working-capital facility. Discuss the project, borrower contribution and permitted costs with the CDC and lender before assuming a purchase fits.

Microloans: smaller financing needs

SBA microloans are made through designated nonprofit intermediary lenders to help small businesses start and expand. They can support eligible working capital, inventory, supplies and equipment; they cannot be used to purchase real estate or pay existing debt. Each intermediary sets its lending requirements. Certain nonprofit childcare centres can also be eligible, an exception to the usual focus on for-profit businesses.

Eligibility basics to discuss before applying

  • For-profit, US-based operations: 7(a) and 504 generally serve eligible businesses operating for profit in the United States. A US registration alone does not resolve every operating, ownership or eligibility question. Ask the lender to review your actual structure and business activity.
  • Small-business size standards: SBA size tests depend on the applicable industry and program. Employee numbers or receipts may matter, and affiliated businesses can affect the assessment. Give the lender a complete ownership chart, including related companies.
  • Owner equity injection: the contribution needed depends on the program, transaction and lender assessment. A startup, acquisition and fixed-asset project can be treated differently. Document the source and availability of owner funds; disclose borrowed contributions and the payments attached to them. Do not assume one percentage applies to every SBA loan.
  • The “credit elsewhere” test: for 7(a), the lender assesses whether the desired credit is available on reasonable terms without the government support. Explain your financing circumstances and provide the records requested. This is not an instruction to collect a fixed number of bank rejection letters.
  • Repayment and credit: the lender evaluates the business case, management, cash flow, existing obligations and relevant credit history. Eligible status alone does not establish that the requested debt is affordable.

Before preparing a full submission, ask about excluded activities, ownership requirements, guarantees and security applicable to your situation. Separate a program condition from a lender's additional credit requirement so you understand what evidence resolves each question.

The document checklist lenders ask for

Use this as a preparation list, then obtain the lender's transaction-specific checklist. Startups will not have the same historical records as established businesses; acquisitions may require records from the seller as well.

  • Business plan: describe the offer, customers, market evidence, management, operations, financing request and repayment source. Explain what will change once the loan is funded and what must happen before revenue starts.
  • Financial projections: prepare connected income statements, cash flow forecasts and balance sheets for the lender's requested period. Include assumptions, a debt schedule and a downside case. Keep proposed loan terms clearly labelled until confirmed.
  • Personal financial statement: supply the form and supporting records requested for relevant owners or guarantors. Show assets, liabilities, contingent obligations and liquidity consistently with the application.
  • Tax returns and historical records: gather requested personal and business returns, available financial statements, interim results and bank records. Explain material differences between tax records, management accounts and the forecast.
  • Entity documents: collect formation or incorporation records, operating agreements or bylaws, ownership details and signing authority. Add relevant licences, leases, management résumés and transaction agreements when requested.
  • Collateral documentation: identify offered assets, ownership, existing liens and outstanding secured debt. Provide valuations, appraisals, insurance or title records as requested. A list of assets does not establish their acceptable security value.
  • Use-of-funds breakdown: itemise project costs with quotes or other support. Match the total uses to proposed borrowing, owner funds and other financing. Identify which sources are confirmed and which remain conditional.

What lenders read for in the plan and projections

The lender needs to trace the request from business purpose to spending and then to repayment. Explain how customer demand becomes sales: units, pricing, capacity, conversion rates and repeat business. Support important assumptions with trading records, contracts, local research or supplier evidence, clearly distinguishing estimates from commitments.

Connect the operating story to the model. Hiring must agree with payroll, equipment purchases with capital spending, and inventory with payment timing. Show cash after operating expenses, taxes, owner needs and debt payments. Profit alone does not show whether cash is available when instalments fall due.

Test a slower sales ramp or delayed opening and explain how the resulting cash gap would be funded. Review our SBA financial package guidance for more detail on linked statements. A longer narrative cannot compensate for financial schedules that contradict each other.

The application process, step by step

  1. Define the request. Set out the business stage, total project cost, purpose, proposed borrowing and available owner funds. Identify timing dependencies such as a lease, purchase agreement or equipment delivery.
  2. Choose a participating lender. For 7(a), apply through an SBA-approved lender, not directly to SBA. SBA's Lender Match tool can help identify participating lenders. For 504, involve a CDC and senior lender; for microloans, contact an approved intermediary.
  3. Confirm fit and the checklist. Ask the lender to review program eligibility, uses, contribution expectations, security and documentation. Resolve major structural questions before developing the full package.
  4. Prepare and reconcile the submission. Complete the forms, plan, projections and supporting records. Check that the borrower name, ownership, request, equity and use of funds agree throughout. Track outstanding items in one list.
  5. Respond during underwriting. The lender assesses credit and repayment, verifies information and follows the applicable SBA processing route. It may ask for revised assumptions or further evidence. SBA involvement depends on the program and the lender's authority.
  6. Meet closing and disbursement conditions. Review the offered terms and required guarantees, security, contribution evidence and legal documents. An approval or conditional offer is distinct from completed closing and release of funds.

Common reasons applications stall

Incomplete projections: a sales forecast without cash flow, opening balances or debt payments leaves repayment unexplained. Reconcile the statements, include the pre-opening period and attach assumptions. Replace unexplained growth with a calculation tied to capacity and customer evidence.

Unclear use of funds: a single line labelled “expansion” gives the lender little to assess. Break it into quoted costs and working-capital needs, remove double-counted items and identify how any ineligible spending will be financed.

Weak equity position: proposed owner funds may be unverified, committed elsewhere or insufficient for the project and cash buffer. Provide a clear trail for the contribution and discuss the financing structure with the lender. Disclose any obligations attached to those funds.

Personal credit issues: unexplained late payments, disputed entries or undisclosed liabilities can trigger follow-up. Review your records early, correct errors through the proper channels and prepare factual explanations with supporting documents. Do not assume a generic credit-score threshold settles the application.

Conflicting versions: a revised quote may change the funding request without reaching the cash flow model. Maintain a dated submission set and update every affected schedule when the project changes. Answer lender questions by referencing the relevant document and assumption.

Frequently asked questions

Do I apply to the SBA or a bank?

For a 7(a) loan, apply through an SBA-approved lender, which may be a bank or another participating lending institution. You do not submit a standard 7(a) borrowing request directly to SBA. For 504 financing, work with a Certified Development Company and a senior lender; for microloans, apply through a designated intermediary.

How much equity do I need for an SBA loan?

There is no single contribution percentage that applies to every SBA loan. The program, transaction type and lender assessment affect what is needed. Ask the lender to confirm the required injection, acceptable funding sources and evidence before committing funds. Keep the contribution separate from the cash needed to operate after closing.

Does SBA require a business plan?

Lenders commonly request a business plan, particularly when a startup or major transaction needs a detailed repayment explanation. SBA does not prescribe one identical business-plan package for every 7(a) application. Confirm the lender’s required narrative, forecast period and attachments, and make sure the plan agrees with the financial submission.

How long does SBA loan approval take?

Timing depends on the program, lender processing route, completeness of the file and transaction complexity. Initial screening, underwriting, any required SBA review, closing and disbursement are separate stages. Missing documents, appraisals or unresolved conditions can extend the process. Ask the lender which stage your file has reached and what remains outstanding.

Can a startup qualify for an SBA loan?

A startup can be considered for SBA financing, subject to the applicable program rules and lender underwriting. Without operating history, the application needs a credible plan, supported projections, relevant management experience and documented funding sources. Discuss eligibility, owner contribution and working-capital needs with the lender; startup status alone does not establish qualification.

Official program resources

These sources describe the programs and application channels; the document preparation suggestions above are general planning guidance.
