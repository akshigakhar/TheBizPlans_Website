Business Plan Preparation for Canadian Business Immigration

An immigration business plan supports the business and economic component of an application or submission; it is not the immigration application itself. It presents the proposed Canadian business, applicant's role and commercially grounded implementation plan in a form suited to the intended decision-maker.

Exact requirements vary by immigration pathway, province, applicant, proposed business, transaction and application stage. The scope and evidence must therefore be tailored rather than drawn from one generic template.

Separate professional services

Business-plan preparation and immigration representation are separate professional services unless specifically included in an engagement. Review the scope of our business plan writing support before choosing the appropriate engagement.

One coherent business case

Consistency Between the Business Plan and the Immigration Application

The business plan should be developed consistently with information elsewhere in the application. Where relevant, that includes the investment amount, ownership, applicant background, proposed role, location, business activities, staffing, implementation timeline, financing and operating assumptions.

We develop the business narrative from verified client information and materials supplied by the applicant and, where applicable, their immigration representative. The applicant remains responsible for verifying the facts; legal and program advice should come from the authorized representative responsible for the application.

Financial Projections for Immigration Business Plans

The financial model should connect investment → launch or acquisition → sales → staffing → operating expenses → capital expenditures → working capital → profitability → cash flow. The numbers should reconcile with the operating plan rather than exist as a separate exercise.

Depending on scope, the model may include projected income statements, cash-flow projections, projected balance sheets, documented assumptions and sensitivity analysis where appropriate.

Helpful answers

Frequently Asked Questions About Immigration Business Plans

What is an immigration business plan?

It is a commercial planning document that supports the business or economic component of an immigration application or submission. It explains the proposed Canadian business, the applicant's role, implementation and financial assumptions; it is not the immigration application itself.

What should an immigration business plan include?

Its scope may include the business concept, ownership and investment, applicant background, market evidence, operations, staffing, implementation, risks and financial projections. The appropriate content depends on the pathway, business and applicant circumstances.

Are immigration business-plan requirements the same for every program?

No. Requirements and emphasis vary by pathway, province, applicant, proposed business, transaction and application stage. A generic plan should not be assumed to suit every application.

Do you prepare financial projections?

Yes. Depending on scope, projections may include income statements, cash-flow forecasts, balance sheets, supporting assumptions, break-even analysis and sensitivity analysis where appropriate.

Can you prepare a plan for a new business or business acquisition?

Yes, when the engagement can be appropriately scoped. A start-up plan addresses launch assumptions and milestones, while an acquisition plan should reflect the proposed transaction, existing operations and post-acquisition strategy.

How is an immigration business plan different from a bank-loan business plan?

Both require commercial and financial credibility. An immigration plan is developed around the applicable business-immigration context and applicant's implementation role, while a bank plan generally places greater emphasis on financing needs, security, repayment and lender assessment.

Does the business plan guarantee immigration approval?

No. A professional plan can present the business case clearly and consistently, but government officers and other authorized organizations make application decisions independently.

Do you provide immigration representation with the business plan?

Business-plan preparation and immigration representation are separate professional services unless representation is specifically included in an engagement.

Financial modelling: connect the investment to operations

The financial model should explain how the venture moves from initial spending to customer receipts while meeting operating obligations. A profitable forecast can still run out of cash if customers pay late, inventory arrives before sales or equipment is purchased at launch. An integrated model makes those timing differences visible. Our financial modelling service can be scoped with the business narrative or separately when the commercial research is already complete.

Separate net worth, available equity and business spending. Personal assets are not automatically liquid launch capital. Document the proposed source of funds, any restrictions and the expected timing of transfers. Distinguish personal support resources from business funds and reconcile the investment schedule with the transaction or start-up budget. A program’s published minimum investment is not a cost estimate for the proposed venture.

Build revenue from operating drivers. For a service business, connect billable capacity, utilization and price. For retail, show transactions, average basket and opening assumptions. For a product venture, document units, pricing, channel economics and commercialization dependencies. Keep estimated orders separate from signed business, and explain seasonal patterns. A reader should be able to see what evidence supports each major driver and what remains an assumption.

Include the full cost of delivery. Model wages, employer costs, rent, utilities, insurance, marketing, supplies, software, professional services and maintenance as applicable. Explain when equipment is paid for and when it becomes available. Consider inventory, receivables and payables rather than treating accounting profit as cash. Income statements, cash flows and balance sheets should reconcile with one another and with the ownership and financing narrative.

Test a downside case. Consider slower customer acquisition, a delayed opening, weaker margins or higher input costs. Explain which spending can be deferred and which commitments remain fixed. The purpose is to understand the cash buffer and management choices, not to manufacture a favourable result. Record the version and assumptions so an applicant can explain why the model changed after new evidence arrived.

Use Canadian business-plan benchmarks to challenge assumptions, then replace broad comparisons with business-specific evidence where available. Our business plan generator can organize an initial draft, but every market statement, financial assumption and program reference still needs review. See financial forecasting guidance for a practical introduction to forecast structure.

A four-step planning process

  1. Define the scope and verify the context

    Discuss the intended pathway, province, business stage, applicant background and reviewer. Check program information before deciding what planning work is useful. Identify whether the project is a start-up, acquisition, expansion or update to an existing file. Confirm deliverables, responsibilities and the distinction between planning and separately engaged representation.

  2. Collect evidence and test the business assumptions

    Gather ownership records, applicant experience, funding evidence, supplier estimates, market research and transaction documents where relevant. Record which information is verified and which is provisional. For an acquisition, distinguish seller-supplied results from independently supported records and explain how the proposed owner intends to operate the business after completion.

  3. Develop the narrative and financial model together

    Prepare the business case and projections in parallel so the staffing, marketing, location and investment schedules agree. Identify unresolved questions early. Explain risks and dependencies in the narrative, then reflect their financial effect in the scenarios. Atul’s CPA and RCIC-IRB perspective helps identify inconsistencies between business evidence and the stated application context.

  4. Reconcile, review and deliver

    The applicant verifies factual statements, proposed responsibilities and available funding. Resolve differences across the plan, supporting documents and model before finalizing the agreed package. Keep a dated final version and an assumptions record for future updates. If a legal or program question arises, it belongs with the authorized representative responsible for that aspect of the file.

Frequently asked questions

What is an immigration business plan?

It explains the proposed Canadian business, applicant’s implementation role, market evidence, funding, staffing and financial assumptions for the relevant reviewer. It supports the commercial component of a file and is not the immigration application itself.

Can I use one plan for every Canadian immigration program?

No. The proposal must reflect the particular pathway, province, business and stage. Check current official requirements before preparing a submission; a generic template cannot establish eligibility.

Is the Start-Up Visa Program open?

IRCC reports that SUV was paused on June 30, 2026 and is not accepting new applications, while previously accepted applications continue to be processed. Checked October 26, 2026.

How much investment does a PNP entrepreneur plan need?

There is no single Canadian minimum. On October 26, 2026, BC published Base thresholds of CAD 600,000 net worth and CAD 200,000 eligible personal investment, and Regional thresholds of CAD 300,000 and CAD 100,000. Alberta Rural published CAD 300,000 net worth and CAD 100,000 equity investment. These are selected criteria, not complete eligibility tests.

Are business-plan writing and immigration representation included together?

They are separate services unless the written engagement specifically includes representation. Atul Jagga’s CPA and RCIC-IRB credentials inform the review, while the engagement defines responsibilities and deliverables.

What should I prepare for a consultation?
