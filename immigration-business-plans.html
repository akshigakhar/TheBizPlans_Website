Business planning, clearly scoped

Business Plan Preparation for Canadian Business Immigration

An immigration business plan supports the business and economic component of an application or submission; it is not the immigration application itself. It presents the proposed Canadian business, applicant's role and commercially grounded implementation plan in a form suited to the intended decision-maker.

Exact requirements vary by immigration pathway, province, applicant, proposed business, transaction and application stage. The scope and evidence must therefore be tailored rather than drawn from one generic template.

Separate professional services

Business-plan preparation and immigration representation are separate professional services unless specifically included in an engagement. Review the scope of our business plan writing support before choosing the appropriate engagement.

One coherent business case

Consistency Between the Business Plan and the Immigration Application

The business plan should be developed consistently with information elsewhere in the application. Where relevant, that includes the investment amount, ownership, applicant background, proposed role, location, business activities, staffing, implementation timeline, financing and operating assumptions.

We develop the business narrative from verified client information and materials supplied by the applicant and, where applicable, their immigration representative. The applicant remains responsible for verifying the facts; legal and program advice should come from the authorized representative responsible for the application.

Narrative and numbers working together

Financial Projections for Immigration Business Plans

The financial model should connect investment → launch or acquisition → sales → staffing → operating expenses → capital expenditures → working capital → profitability → cash flow. The numbers should reconcile with the operating plan rather than exist as a separate exercise.

Depending on scope, the model may include projected income statements, cash-flow projections, projected balance sheets, documented assumptions and sensitivity analysis where appropriate.

Helpful answers

Frequently Asked Questions About Immigration Business Plans

What is an immigration business plan?

It is a commercial planning document that supports the business or economic component of an immigration application or submission. It explains the proposed Canadian business, the applicant's role, implementation and financial assumptions; it is not the immigration application itself.

What should an immigration business plan include?

Its scope may include the business concept, ownership and investment, applicant background, market evidence, operations, staffing, implementation, risks and financial projections. The appropriate content depends on the pathway, business and applicant circumstances.

Are immigration business-plan requirements the same for every program?

No. Requirements and emphasis vary by pathway, province, applicant, proposed business, transaction and application stage. A generic plan should not be assumed to suit every application.

Do you prepare financial projections?

Yes. Depending on scope, projections may include income statements, cash-flow forecasts, balance sheets, supporting assumptions, break-even analysis and sensitivity analysis where appropriate.

Can you prepare a plan for a new business or business acquisition?

Yes, when the engagement can be appropriately scoped. A start-up plan addresses launch assumptions and milestones, while an acquisition plan should reflect the proposed transaction, existing operations and post-acquisition strategy.

How is an immigration business plan different from a bank-loan business plan?

Both require commercial and financial credibility. An immigration plan is developed around the applicable business-immigration context and applicant's implementation role, while a bank plan generally places greater emphasis on financing needs, security, repayment and lender assessment.

Does the business plan guarantee immigration approval?

No. A professional plan can present the business case clearly and consistently, but government officers and other authorized organizations make application decisions independently.

Do you provide immigration representation with the business plan?

Business-plan preparation and immigration representation are separate professional services unless representation is specifically included in an engagement.
