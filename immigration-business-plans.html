Choose the program context before drafting

Canadian business-immigration routes serve different purposes. A provincial entrepreneur proposal, an innovative start-up file and a temporary work-permit expansion plan should not be treated as interchangeable submissions. Confirm the responsible authority, current intake, intended applicant role and stage before commissioning research or committing money. Published minimums describe only part of a program; they are not a finding that a particular person or business qualifies.

The cards below summarize selected official information checked on October 26, 2026. Requirements and intake can change after that date. Follow the official links and discuss individual circumstances in a consultation. Where a current official page could not be verified, we omit figures rather than recycling historic thresholds.

Canadian immigration program planning cards

Start-up Visa: existing-file planning

Program status — checked October 26, 2026: IRCC reports that the Start-Up Visa Program was paused on June 30, 2026 and is not accepting new applications. Previously accepted applications continue to be processed. Read IRCC’s current SUV status (checked October 26, 2026). Planning content below is relevant to existing files and commercial planning; it does not announce reopened intake.

SUV planning focuses on an innovative venture, its founders, Canadian operations, commercialization and financial capacity. For an existing file, a useful update separates the original assumptions from actual progress, explains material changes and links the current financial model to the business evidence. IRCC’s designated-organization list (checked October 26, 2026) identifies the organization types. Our Start-up Visa business-plan page explains the planning work and letter-of-support context.

Ontario OINP Entrepreneur: verify availability first

Entrepreneur planning for Ontario can involve starting or purchasing a business and explaining the applicant’s active management role. However, we could not retrieve the live Ontario program pages during this build; current intake and requirements are unverified here. Do not rely on historic Entrepreneur Stream thresholds or assume the stream is open. Consult the official Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program page and obtain an individual assessment before proceeding. No Ontario net-worth or investment figures are published on this hub. Our Ontario planning resource provides background that must also be checked against current official information.

BC PNP Entrepreneur Immigration

BC’s Base stream is aimed at entrepreneurs establishing a new business or taking over and growing an existing one. Its Regional stream focuses on a new business in a participating community outside the Metro Vancouver Regional District. BC publishes minimum personal net worth of CAD 600,000 for Base and CAD 300,000 for Regional, with eligible personal investment of CAD 200,000 and CAD 100,000, respectively. Official WelcomeBC entrepreneur requirements (checked October 26, 2026). These are selected financial criteria; business eligibility, experience, community requirements and other conditions also matter. A BC PNP business plan should explain the local market, owner’s role, implementation and the evidence behind the proposed investment.

Alberta AAIP entrepreneur streams

AAIP has distinct entrepreneur pathways; the Rural Entrepreneur Stream is for entrepreneurs starting a new business or buying an existing business in a rural Alberta community. Alberta publishes minimum net worth of CAD 300,000 and minimum business investment of CAD 100,000 from the candidate’s own and/or spouse or common-law partner’s equity. Official Alberta Rural Entrepreneur eligibility (checked October 26, 2026). Those figures apply to this stream, not every AAIP entrepreneur route. Community support and other criteria also apply. Our AAIP business-plan resource can help frame local demand, acquisition assumptions, operational responsibilities and a sustainable funding schedule.

C11 / ICT work-permit plans

Temporary business-owner and intra-company-transfer cases require their own analysis. C11 planning should connect the owner’s actual work and temporary purpose to a supported Canadian benefit. IRCC’s C11 business-owner instructions (checked October 26, 2026). ICT planning should explain the foreign and Canadian enterprises, the relationship between them, the proposed position and operational readiness. IRCC’s ICT instructions (checked October 26, 2026). A plan cannot create a qualifying corporate relationship or substitute for evidence of the applicant’s experience. Review our C11 / owner-operator planning page and ICT business-plan resource. Work-permit planning should not be presented as an automatic permanent-residence route.

What immigration officers look for in a plan

The evidence required depends on the pathway. As a planning principle, the reader should be able to follow the business from concept to implementation without guessing who performs the work or where the funding comes from. IRCC’s C11 guidance specifically discusses concrete start-up actions and commercial viability. Read the official C11 assessment guidance (checked October 26, 2026). The points below organize the commercial work; they are not a universal immigration eligibility checklist.

A specific customer and local opportunity. Describe the buyer, the need, the location and competing options. A national industry statistic is useful context but does not establish demand for a particular shop, consultancy or technology product. Explain how local pricing, travel patterns, purchasing behaviour or procurement practices affect the launch. Identify evidence gaps before converting assumptions into a sales forecast.

A credible applicant role. Connect the founder’s documented experience to day-to-day responsibilities. Explain which decisions the applicant makes, what staff or contractors do and which professional licences or permissions the operation may need. A biography is stronger when it supports an operating role and identifies where additional expertise will be required. Do not invent qualifications or imply that an incorporation document authorizes regulated work.

Implementation that matches the proposal. Show premises needs, supplier relationships, equipment, technology, hiring and launch dependencies. Label a potential lease as potential, and distinguish a customer conversation from a signed contract. Use an evidence register with the document date, source and implication for the plan. The objective is a consistent account of what exists now and what still needs to happen.

Supported economic benefits. Explain how projected jobs, procurement, services, productivity or exports arise from actual activities. Tie a hiring plan to sales capacity and payroll funding. Identify the local relevance of the proposal rather than assuming every business creates a significant benefit. Avoid counting the same projected expenditure as both invested capital and an additional source of cash.

Financial modelling: connect the investment to operations

The financial model should explain how the venture moves from initial spending to customer receipts while meeting operating obligations. A profitable forecast can still run out of cash if customers pay late, inventory arrives before sales or equipment is purchased at launch. An integrated model makes those timing differences visible. Our financial modelling service can be scoped with the business narrative or separately when the commercial research is already complete.

Separate net worth, available equity and business spending. Personal assets are not automatically liquid launch capital. Document the proposed source of funds, any restrictions and the expected timing of transfers. Distinguish personal support resources from business funds and reconcile the investment schedule with the transaction or start-up budget. A program’s published minimum investment is not a cost estimate for the proposed venture.

Build revenue from operating drivers. For a service business, connect billable capacity, utilization and price. For retail, show transactions, average basket and opening assumptions. For a product venture, document units, pricing, channel economics and commercialization dependencies. Keep estimated orders separate from signed business, and explain seasonal patterns. A reader should be able to see what evidence supports each major driver and what remains an assumption.

Include the full cost of delivery. Model wages, employer costs, rent, utilities, insurance, marketing, supplies, software, professional services and maintenance as applicable. Explain when equipment is paid for and when it becomes available. Consider inventory, receivables and payables rather than treating accounting profit as cash. Income statements, cash flows and balance sheets should reconcile with one another and with the ownership and financing narrative.

Test a downside case. Consider slower customer acquisition, a delayed opening, weaker margins or higher input costs. Explain which spending can be deferred and which commitments remain fixed. The purpose is to understand the cash buffer and management choices, not to manufacture a favourable result. Record the version and assumptions so an applicant can explain why the model changed after new evidence arrived.

Use Canadian business-plan benchmarks to challenge assumptions, then replace broad comparisons with business-specific evidence where available. Our business plan generator can organize an initial draft, but every market statement, financial assumption and program reference still needs review. A four-step planning process

  1. Define the scope and verify the context

    Discuss the intended pathway, province, business stage, applicant background and reviewer. Check program information before deciding what planning work is useful. Identify whether the project is a start-up, acquisition, expansion or update to an existing file. Confirm deliverables, responsibilities and the distinction between planning and separately engaged representation.

  2. Collect evidence and test the business assumptions

    Gather ownership records, applicant experience, funding evidence, supplier estimates, market research and transaction documents where relevant. Record which information is verified and which is provisional. For an acquisition, distinguish seller-supplied results from independently supported records and explain how the proposed owner intends to operate the business after completion.

  3. Develop the narrative and financial model together

    Prepare the business case and projections in parallel so the staffing, marketing, location and investment schedules agree. Identify unresolved questions early. Explain risks and dependencies in the narrative, then reflect their financial effect in the scenarios. Atul’s CPA and RCIC-IRB perspective helps identify inconsistencies between business evidence and the stated application context.

  4. Reconcile, review and deliver

    The applicant verifies factual statements, proposed responsibilities and available funding. Resolve differences across the plan, supporting documents and model before finalizing the agreed package. Keep a dated final version and an assumptions record for future updates. If a legal or program question arises, it belongs with the authorized representative responsible for that aspect of the file.

Prepare for a useful consultation

Bring a concise business description, target location, proposed ownership and your relevant experience. If you are purchasing a business, identify which financial records and transaction terms are available. If you are expanding an overseas company, explain its current operations and the intended Canadian role. Tell us which documents have already been submitted so revisions can be discussed in that context.

You do not need to turn uncertain ideas into firm claims before the first conversation. It is more useful to identify missing evidence, funding questions and operational dependencies openly. We can then discuss whether the immediate need is research, a complete plan, financial projections or a review of an existing draft. Frequently asked questions

What is an immigration business plan?

It explains the proposed Canadian business, applicant’s implementation role, market evidence, funding, staffing and financial assumptions for the relevant reviewer. It supports the commercial component of a file and is not the immigration application itself.

Can I use one plan for every Canadian immigration program?

No. The proposal must reflect the particular pathway, province, business and stage. Check current official requirements before preparing a submission; a generic template cannot establish eligibility.

Is the Start-Up Visa Program open?

IRCC reports that SUV was paused on June 30, 2026 and is not accepting new applications, while previously accepted applications continue to be processed. Checked October 26, 2026.

How much investment does a PNP entrepreneur plan need?

There is no single Canadian minimum. On October 26, 2026, BC published Base thresholds of CAD 600,000 net worth and CAD 200,000 eligible personal investment, and Regional thresholds of CAD 300,000 and CAD 100,000. Alberta Rural published CAD 300,000 net worth and CAD 100,000 equity investment. These are selected criteria, not complete eligibility tests.

Are business-plan writing and immigration representation included together?

They are separate services unless the written engagement specifically includes representation. Atul Jagga’s CPA and RCIC-IRB credentials inform the review, while the engagement defines responsibilities and deliverables.

What should I prepare for a consultation?

Bring your proposed pathway, province, business concept or acquisition details, ownership, background, available funding evidence and existing records. Identify the reviewer and application stage so the planning scope can be discussed.
